## Timeline

- 2026-08-23 (22:02-22:06) — Question about doc section 4.3 ("Why keep
  two copies of K instead of just storing K transposed?"): did the FPGA
  transpose K for the pre-AMX software path? Answer, verified against the
  tron-amx source: no on both parts. The software path never needed a
  materialized K transpose — the AVX-512 kernel computes S = Q·Kᵀ as one
  dot product per stored K row, and writing result i into score slot s[i]
  performs the transpose through loop indexing alone (zero bytes
  rearranged; self_attention.hpp:1568/1589, dotter.hpp:176). The FPGA
  cannot be the explanation: with FPGA attention on, the CPU computes its
  disjoint share of scores entirely from the K pages in the KV cache, and
  no intermediate result flows back from the card into the CPU's QK
  computation. AMX's TDPBF16PS tile instruction is the first consumer
  that needs the transposed layout to exist physically in memory — that
  is why the mirror copy exists.
- 2026-08-24 (09:09-09:20) — On the user's request, added section
  "4.4 Why no K transpose was needed before AMX (jhan's question)" to the
  doc, directly after section 4.3. The doc is generated, so the edit went
