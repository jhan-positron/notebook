The user was surprised that the campaign report says 10 % of gpt-oss-120b's decode keys run in software under FPGA attention. ## Results

All numbers are from the 2026-10-02 campaign files. Nothing new was run.

Decode K tokens of gpt-oss-120b at prompt 1,024, 8 users, FPGA attention [rt/gptoss-8u-p1024-fpga__fpga__amxon__rep1.log line 193, identical in the amxoff run]:

| Who scores, which layers | K tokens | Share of decode K tokens |
|---|---|---|
| card, 18 card layers (odd layers 1 to 35) | 339,148,800 | 89.81 % |
| CPU, 18 sliding-window layers (even layers 0 to 34) | 37,748,736 | 10.00 % |
| CPU, tail past the last complete GOF, 18 card layers | 737,280 | 0.20 % |

Per decode step, per KV head, per layer: a card layer scores about 1,152 keys on the card and (p mod 4) + 1 keys (mean 2.5) on the CPU. A sliding-window layer scores its 128-key window on the CPU and nothing on the card. The CPU share by keys is (18 x 128 + 18 x 2.5) / (18 x 128 + 18 x 1,152.5) = 10.19 %, which matches the report's 10.2 %.

Why the card skips the sliding-window layers: a layer with a finite sliding window runs every visible key in software whatever the offload setting [h/tron/models/self_attention.hpp:1406-1410 at 3b16a74966; h/tron/models/ranged_mask.hpp:152-153].

Time budget of one decode forward, gpt-oss, prompt 1,024, 8 users, amxon run, per-layer figures from the in-flight snapshot covering about 100 of the 256 decode forwards [leaves/.../decode_like_layer_*]:

| Item | Per forward, per attention worker | Share of the 10.26 ms forward (T1) |
|---|---|---|
| attention busy (T2) in the 18 sliding-window layers | 0.649 ms (36.0 us per job) | 6.3 % |
| join wait (T5) inside it, unchanged by any kernel | 0.119 ms (6.6 us per job) | 1.2 % |
| scoring, softmax and PV, the part a kernel could shorten | 0.530 ms | 5.2 % |
| keys a kernel could take in those layers (dense pages only) | 50.9 % of their K tokens | 5.1 % of all decode K tokens |

A kernel takes dense pages only (all 64 entries live, fully visible, whole page inside the window) [self_attention.hpp:1596-1618]. A 128-key window covers one full page plus two partial ones, so one page per step qualifies.

Estimates (labelled est., from section 7 of the compare report, kernel gains on the fitting models):

| Case | Basis | Estimated gain |
|---|---|---|
| decode, realistic | fitting models cut decode busy per job 12 to 16 % while scoring 96 to 99 % of keys; half the keys at that rate | 0.04 to 0.05 ms per forward, 0.4 to 0.5 % of T1 (est.) |
| decode, ceiling | the whole 5.2 % scoring part at zero cost | 5.2 % of T1 (measured bound) |
| prefill (TTFT) | sliding-window busy 29.0 ms per worker per 335 ms prompt forward (8.7 %) plus card layers' own-chunk part 23.1 ms (6.9 %); fitting models cut prefill busy 1.7x to 2.5x; half the keys | 9 to 13 ms, 3 to 4 % of TTFT (est.) |

Both estimates assume the attention busy time is on the critical path of the layer. The per-key gain in a 128-key window is not measured (the window's K and V, 16 KB each per KV head per step, sit in cache). Insufficient data there. Only a built kernel can measure it.

## Gotchas and decisions

- The 10 % counts keys scored, not time. In time the sliding-window attention is 6.3 % of a decode forward.
- The user's tail rule ("1 of 4 new tokens on FPGA") is not what the counters show. The CPU tail in a card layer is (p mod 4) + 1 keys per step, the keys written since the last complete GOF.
- The per-layer timings come from the in-flight FUSE snapshot, which covers about 100 of the 256 decode forwards, not the exit report.
