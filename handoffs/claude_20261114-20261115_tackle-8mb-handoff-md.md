## Short version

The session executed `tackle-8mb-handoff.md`: it ran four test rounds on CRB (an Arm reference server) to find out why the new read kernel is 27 % faster than the old one at a 1 MiB buffer and only about 1 % faster at 8 MiB. The answer: the new loop pays about 0.66 extra processor cycles for each 64-byte line that comes from the shared cache, so at 8 MiB it costs almost as much as the old loop, which needs about 2.67 cycles per line from 1 MiB to 16 MiB. The session wrote the HTML report, a plain-English rewrite of it, an easy-read page and a result handoff, and it left two questions unanswered because they need root.

### The answer to the 16:17 question (chat only)

- Old read is limited by the work inside its own loop (about 2.67 cycles per line, 67 GB/s). It runs at 66.7 GB/s even at 32 KiB, where the data is in L1.
- L2 and the SLC both deliver lines faster than that up to 8 MiB. The new loop shows the supply: 85 GB/s from L2 and 69.0 to 71.5 GB/s at 8 MiB.
- Hypothesis of the session, not tested: the cost per line is the larger of "loop work" and "supply time", not their sum, because the prefetcher overlaps the wait with the loop's work.
- Not explained by this hypothesis: at 16 MiB old read keeps 66.9 to 67.1 GB/s while the new loop gets 65.7 to 66.2 GB/s. Old read also dips to 63.6 GB/s in some processes at 2.5 MiB.
