# cache-mem-perf on the Asimov emulator: run membench-jibin-004 (round three)

Jibin Han's `cache-mem-perf` (github.com/jhan-positron/asimov, folder `cache-mem-perf`, commit `ba834c0af665f4730eae75b544d4a0227fe36dcc`) ran on one emulated Asimov hemisphere on the GA13 Palladium on 2026-11-03 (UTC): his cache test and then his new CME test, as one job (`sh run_all.sh`). His whole results directory is `results/19700101T000000Z/`, byte for byte as his scripts wrote it; `SHA256SUMS` lists each file's sha256. The run's evidence is `evidence/runs/membench-jibin-004/` in the emuMemBenchBox sandbox, and the scripts behind this README's derived numbers are in `evidence/jibin/r3/`. Round two is `../membench-jibin-002/`, its control run `../membench-jibin-003/`.

## Read first

- **The platform is round two's.** Same image copy, firmware (SCP 0.48.0 with PCIe0 held, TF-A 0.35.0), kernel and OCM module; only this box's init and his programs changed. The init's two changes leave his measurements alone: a hang guard, which would have stopped his scripts 12 chip seconds after they started and still returned everything they wrote (it did not fire: they ran 2.918 s), and room for 256 files in the results window (he wrote 80).
- **His cache test repeats round two.** Of the best rows of the commands both rounds ran, 33 of 36 latency rows agree within 0.73% (the other three within 2.6%), every bandwidth row within 2.3%, and the multi-core DRAM writes read 127.9-155.2 GB/s, as in round two (127.9-155.4). One shift came with his rebuilt `bandwidth`: the one-core write rows of 16-256 KiB take a steady 7.7-8.0 ns more per pass.
- **The new sparse OCM rows** read 2.3 ns above round two's dense rows on average and spread 5-15 ns over their 7 passes, but within each CPU they still order by mesh distance; the dense all-five control repeats round two within 0.17 ns.
- **Through the CME, a dependent load costs 21.4 ns at 4 KiB,** where a register round trip through the CME with no load costs 21.0 ns. It climbs from 8 KiB on (24.2 ns at 8 KiB, 47.6 at 64 KiB, 53.1 at 256 KiB, 70.3 at 32 MiB), with no clear step at the CME L1's 64 KiB. OCM reads 35.5-38.5 ns per region through the CME, against 21.4-25.8 ns for CPU 2's own loads.
- **One core streams about 37 GB/s of reads and 62-64 GB/s of writes through the CME** from 160 KiB to 32 MiB, about 4 and 2 times its own rates; the write rate is flat from 16 KiB to 2 MiB.
- **All three CME counters count,** 0x327D and 0x327E included, which no asimov-bench code opens and no earlier run here read: both read zero in the no-memory control rows; 0x327E reads nonzero wherever the CME loads memory, and 0x327D in every DRAM row (on the OCM passes, whose lines come from outside the cluster, it reads 0-0.03 per load). But 0x327E reads above one refill per load in most DRAM rows, and the CME already refills from outside the cluster at 8 and 16 KiB (1.65-3.43 and 6.55-9.27 per load), which a 64 KiB L1 should hold. That, and the climb from 8 KiB, are his to look into; this run's data cannot settle them.
- **If the CME powers down between his timed spans, waking costs at most about 0.3 us a span in all but one of them**, up to about 5 ns per hop on a 64-hop OCM pass and under 1 ns on the longer passes; the one, the first `cme-call` rep of the all-five OCM file, ran 2.5 us long while the CME counted only 419 more cycles, which a power-up stall would fit (inference; CME power, below).

## Platform facts

These equal round two's (`../membench-jibin-002/README.md`, Platform facts), read on its qualification boot `membench-boot-003`, which ran the same image copy, firmware, kernel and OCM module; this run's snapshot differs from round two's only in this box's init and his programs, and its device tree only in where the initial ramdisk ends (`evidence/bundle/membench-jibin-ba834c0-fw19-checks.txt` and `membench-jibin-ba834c0-fw19-2-checks.txt`). Linux saw the same platform: this run's `sysinfo.txt` equals round two's apart from MemFree, timestamps and three boot-memory lines for the slightly larger initial ramdisk. The CME lines are new. The sources are in `results/platform-facts.md` in the sandbox.

- **Emulator image:** GA13 release `a10280100`, build `hemi_rtws_tlm`, RTL `0d9e214eb43b` (main, 2026-10-28), from this box's private copy. Real C1-Nano cores and clusters with their CMEs, the CMN-S3 mesh with its SLC and OCM, the SCP and the scratchpad; the matmul array is cut to one column. The CMN's HRA leg (0x8000_0000-0xBFFF_FFFF: PCIe and the control registers of SMS, XMS, the scratchpad and others) is tied off on this image, so nothing there answers.
- **Firmware:** SCP `scp-v0.48.0` rebuilt by this box with PCIe0 held (version `0.48.0-nopcie.1`, commit `e361a83`; loaded as the debug build) and TF-A `tfa-v0.35.0` (`3b3df18`) debug, round two's set (what it adds over round one's is in round two's README). SAM profiles `bootloader-no-sam` and `cold-boot-ocm-w4`.
- **Kernel:** Linux 6.18.36 built from asimov-os `v0.0.17`'s config and patches plus transparent huge pages (madvise), hugetlbfs and debugfs; `v0.0.17`'s userspace (busybox 1.37.0, glibc 2.40). This run's banner and command line are in its `dmesg.txt`.
- **CPU placement:** ten C1-Nano cores, all online at boot. Device interrupts default to CPUs 0-1; CPUs 2-9 are isolated from the scheduler and `nohz_full`, with their RCU callbacks offloaded (`MB LAYOUT ok online=0-9 iso=2-9 nohz=2-9`). Each CPU still takes its own timer and per-CPU interrupts, and his programs' system calls and page faults run on their own CPU. His scripts ran on CPU 2; CPU 3 was offline for phases A to D, back for phase E, offline again for the whole of phase F and back at its end. Each pair {2,3}, {4,5}, {6,7}, {8,9} shares an L2, an L3, a CME and one mesh port.
- **Clock:** core 2.000 GHz against the 1 GHz generic timer (one timer tick is one chip nanosecond); his own rows agree, at 4.01 cycles per 2.002 ns L1 load. The CME ran on the same clock: CME cycles per core cycle read 0.9989-1.0000 on every CME latency row of 8,192 loads or more.
- **Caches:** L1 data 32 KiB and L1 instruction 32 KiB per core, L2 128 KiB and L3 256 KiB (one slice) per two-core cluster, 64-byte lines (this run's `sysinfo.txt`), equal to the RTL's configuration. The SLC is five home nodes (HN-S) of 1 MiB in 16 ways each; the firmware locks 4 ways of each as OCM, so 3.75 MiB of SLC caches DRAM. Address translation: a 16-entry L1 data TLB per core and an L2 TLB of at least 1,024 entries (the RTL), so once his buffers have been touched his timed passes should need no page walks (inference from the sizes).
- **CME:** each cluster's two cores share one CME, the C1-SME2 unit that runs their streaming-mode instructions, with its own L1 data cache of 64 KiB in 4 ways (1,024 lines) and its own 24-entry TLB, each entry mapping a 4 KiB, 16 KiB, 64 KiB or 2 MiB page (the RTL: `griffin0_shared_defines.sv:105-109,1260-1261`, `griffin0_tlb.sv:1400-1412`; Linux lists no CME cache). The kernel found SME2 with a 64-byte streaming vector length, as his kernels require; `dmesg.txt`'s SME lines are `[    0.132700] CPU features: detected: Scalable Matrix Extension`, `[    0.134102] CPU features: detected: SME2`, `[    0.146130] SME: minimum available vector length 64 bytes per vector`, `[    0.147680] SME: maximum available vector length 64 bytes per vector` and `[    0.149234] SME: default vector length 64 bytes per vector`, and `/proc/cpuinfo` lists `sme2` on all ten CPUs. The SCP set every cluster's CME power controller to dynamic mode as each of the cluster's cores powered on at boot (`[ASIMOV SYSTEM] CLUSn CME0 PPU PWPR=0x100 (dynamic; SME2 ready)`, twice for each of CLUS0-4, at 0.304-0.505 s on its clock), and set CPU 2-3's again each time CPU 3 came back online: `[    2.014926] [ASIMOV SYSTEM] CLUS1 CME0 PPU PWPR=0x100 (dynamic; SME2 ready)` and `[    3.614926] [ASIMOV SYSTEM] CLUS1 CME0 PPU PWPR=0x100 (dynamic; SME2 ready)`. CPU 3 going offline printed nothing. In dynamic mode the CME switches itself off when idle (CME power, below).
- **Mesh placement:** a 6 by 6 grid of crosspoints. The home nodes of OCM0-4 (node ids 88, 160, 216, 288, 344) sit at crosspoints (x, y) (1,3), (2,4), (3,3), (4,4) and (5,3), and the mesh ports of the clusters of CPUs 2-3, 4-5, 6-7 and 8-9 at (2,3), (3,2), (4,3) and (5,2); the distance from a port to a home node, in crosspoints, is |dx| + |dy|.
- **OCM:** five regions of 256 KiB at 0xE0000000 + n MiB, each in a different home node. `/dev/ocm` is this box's own module (`membench_ocm.ko`), which maps a region as Normal write-back in 4 KiB pages; a future asimov-os driver may map it differently. Nothing initialises OCM; a line never written reads as zeros (no ECC error).
- **Huge pages and memory:** 256 pages of 2 MiB reserved at boot (`init.log`: `huge=256/256`); MemTotal 1,833,848 kB including that pool, as in round two.
- **Core PMU:** `armv9_c1_nano`, 20 event counters plus the cycle counter. His group (cycles and three raw events) counts user space only: 0x03, 0x17 and 0xA2 (L1D, L2D and L3D read refill) on the core rows, 0x3246, 0x327D and 0x327E on the `--cme` rows.
- **Mesh PMU:** `arm_cmn_0`, one DTC for the whole mesh and one DTM per crosspoint. His seven counters fit at once, and none was multiplexed: `running_ns` equals `enabled_ns` on every row of `cmn-counts.csv`.
- **DRAM caveat:** AP DRAM on this image is an embedded emulator memory, not LPDDR5X, so no DRAM latency or bandwidth number here is silicon-like.

## CME power

- **What the RTL says** (`evidence/jibin/cme-power-rtl.md`, read from the RTL, not measured): in dynamic mode the CME switches itself off after about 1.2-1.5 us with both its cores out of streaming mode (256 idle CME clocks, then a 1,024-tick timed wait: the reset values of its power register, which nothing on this platform writes), cleaning and invalidating its L1 as it goes; switching back on invalidates the L1 again. Every timed span of his follows a gap outside streaming mode (perf calls, output, often a flush), and his programs do not time those gaps, so whether the CME was off at the start of a span is unknown.
- **What his rows bound:** three views give about the same fixed CME cost per timed span, 0.1-0.3 us. Averaged over the five regions, the 64-hop sparse OCM passes read 1.2 (bests) to 3.0 (pass means) ns per hop above the 320-hop ones through the CME, about 0.1-0.24 us per pass, where the core's same pair shows none; inside each span his elapsed time, at 2 cycles per ns, exceeds the CME's cycle count by about 400 cycles (0.2 us, about 80 of them the two clock reads), after a flush or not (his cycles column, which also counts about 180 user cycles outside his clock bracket, gives about 600); and each 65,536-hop round-trip rep runs at most 0.4 us beyond its hops. That cost is up to about 5 ns per hop on a 64-hop OCM pass, 0.3-1 ns on a 320-hop pass and invisible on the longer passes; his `cme-call` row carries it once per 64 calls, a few ns per call. So if the CME does switch off between spans, waking costs at most about that; a microsecond-scale wake would show and does not, except once: the first `cme-call` rep of the all-five OCM file took 2.5 us longer (82.6 ns per call), with 5,033 more core cycles and only 419 more CME cycles, which a stall while the CME powers up would fit (inference).
- **The bandwidth write rows** carry about 0.24 us once per rep beyond the two clock reads, and none per pass, where a wake-up after each rep's warm-up barrier would land, but these rows cannot tell it from the first `SMSTART` or the first stores after the barrier.
