# cache-mem-perf on the Asimov emulator: run membench-jibin-001

Jibin Han's `cache-mem-perf` (github.com/jhan-positron/asimov, folder `cache-mem-perf`, commit `9c6b8e747cb169e566769f812371fc9a7f0bac2b`) ran on one emulated Asimov hemisphere on the GA13 Palladium on 2026-10-31. His whole results directory is `results/19700101T000000Z/`, byte for byte as his script wrote it; `SHA256SUMS` lists each file's sha256. The run's evidence is `evidence/runs/membench-jibin-001/` in the emuMemBenchBox sandbox.

## The run

- Queue request 4457 on ga13pd01 (boards 0-3), granted 21:00 UTC. The init started 21:25:14 UTC, his script ran from 21:25:40 to 22:22:59 UTC, and the emulator was released at 22:24 UTC.
- His script ran as he asked: `sh run_asimov.sh` as root, with `latency` and `bandwidth` beside it. This box's init (PID 1) started it as `/bin/sh /opt/membench/cache-mem-perf-9c6b8e7/run_asimov.sh`, pinned to CPU 2 under SCHED_FIFO priority 10, with RT throttling off and no time limit. Every command and thread it started inherited SCHED_FIFO 10; his programs then pin themselves to the CPUs their options name, and the bandwidth program's main thread stays on CPU 2.
- It exited 0 after 1.237 s of chip time (`MB RUN end status=exit:0 ns=1237393226`), and all 34 of his measurement commands exited 0. All 26 of his files came back intact through the results window, which held 29 files with this box's `init.log`, his script's console output (`out.txt`) and the kernel log (`MB WIN ok ... files=29 trunc=0`); the window's checksum matched the init's.
- Build: his Makefile with the asimov-os `v0.0.17` SDK's GCC (Arm GNU Toolchain 14.2.Rel1, 14.2.1) and his default flags: `-O2 -mcpu=cortex-a520 -static -Wall -Wextra -std=gnu11 -pthread -fno-tree-loop-distribute-patterns`. The SDK's toolchain wrapper adds its sysroot, `-mabi=lp64`, `-fstack-protector-strong` and link flags; the stack protector touches only the prologues and epilogues of `main`, `timed_walk` and `worker`, none of the timed loops. `latency` sha256 `68c4d3ddc3c89ff10bed8f7819e08dada4536abcce85250a3c72c9d90f17b002`, `bandwidth` sha256 `d7cf54ddc5fc7fd4db215bb166b1c54b6e45ee6cb84cde2f775c1c63bf18df39`, both static.
- The run's checker passed every criterion (`JIBIN_CHECK PASS`, `evidence/runs/membench-jibin-001/judge-jibin.txt`): the firmware, kernel, CPU layout, huge pages and `/dev/ocm` as qualified; his script's exit 0; all 26 expected files; no nonzero exit among his 34 measurement commands; CPU 3 offline and back; perf counters open in every latency file; every backing his programs print is `hugetlb` (DRAM) or `ocm` (latency prints one per buffer, bandwidth only its first thread's first buffer; the others had room, with 256 pages free and at most 128 needed); no measurement command wrote to stderr (the expected `ls: /dev/asimov-spm` line is in `sysinfo.txt`).
- The emulator ran 2,779 wall seconds per chip second.

## Platform facts

These were read on the qualification boot `membench-boot-002` (GA13 request 4446, 2026-10-31), which ran the same image copy, firmware, kernel and OCM module (this run's snapshot differs from its only in the init and his program), and from the RTL and address map, except where this run's files are cited. The sources are in `results/platform-facts.md`, and the full environment dump in `results/env-membench-boot-002.txt`.

- **Emulator image:** GA13 release `a10280100`, build `hemi_rtws_tlm`, RTL `0d9e214eb43b` (main, 2026-10-28), from this box's private copy. Real C1-Nano cores and clusters, the CMN-S3 mesh with its SLC and OCM, the SCP and the scratchpad; the matmul array is cut to one column. The CMN's HRA leg (0x8000_0000-0xBFFF_FFFF: PCIe and the control registers of SMS, XMS, the scratchpad and others) is tied off on this image, so nothing there answers.
- **Firmware:** SCP `scp-v0.34.0` (`45b053c`) release and TF-A `tfa-v0.24.0` (`cc13f8c`) debug, the firmware of asimov-os `v0.0.13`, with SAM profiles `bootloader-no-sam` (BL1) and `cold-boot-ocm-w4` (BL2). 0.34.0 lacks 0.46.0's later mesh setup (HN-S-to-SMS route overrides, HN-S queue caps) and its changes to the physical SAM programming. The stock `v0.0.17` firmware (SCP 0.46.0) did not boot this image (`membench-boot-001`): its SCP stalled before starting the AP cores, most likely on its first PCIe register read, which this image never answers.
- **Kernel:** Linux 6.18.36 built from asimov-os `v0.0.17`'s config and patches plus transparent huge pages (madvise) and hugetlbfs; `v0.0.17`'s userspace (busybox 1.37.0, glibc 2.40). This run's banner and command line are in its `dmesg.txt`.
- **CPU placement:** ten C1-Nano cores, all online at boot. Device interrupts default to CPUs 0-1; CPUs 2-9 are isolated from the scheduler and `nohz_full`, with their RCU callbacks offloaded (this run's `init.log`: `MB LAYOUT ok online=0-9 iso=2-9 nohz=2-9`). Each CPU still takes its own timer and per-CPU interrupts, and his programs' system calls and page faults run on their own CPU. His script ran on CPU 2; CPU 3 was offline for phases A to D (so CPU 2 had its L2 and L3 alone) and back online for phase E. Each pair {2,3}, {4,5}, {6,7}, {8,9} shares an L2 and an L3, so the per-DSU tests on CPUs 2, 4, 6 and 8 use one core per cluster.
- **Clock:** core 2.000 GHz against the 1 GHz generic timer (one timer tick is one chip nanosecond); his own rows agree, at 4.01 cycles per 2.002 ns L1 load, and his CSV headers read `cntfrq_hz=1000000000`.
- **Caches:** L1 data 32 KiB and L1 instruction 32 KiB per core, L2 128 KiB and L3 256 KiB (one slice) per two-core cluster, 64-byte lines (this run's `sysinfo.txt`); these equal the RTL's configuration. The SLC is five home nodes (HN-S) of 1 MiB in 16 ways each; the firmware locks 4 ways of each as OCM, so 3.75 MiB of SLC caches DRAM.
- **OCM:** five regions of 256 KiB at 0xE0000000 + n MiB, each in a different home node. `/dev/ocm` is this box's own module (`membench_ocm.ko`; no driver in this kernel serves OCM), which maps a region as Normal write-back in 4 KiB pages; a future asimov-os driver may map it differently. CPU 2's cluster port sits one mesh crosspoint from the homes of OCM0-2 and three from OCM3-4. Nothing initialises OCM; a line never written reads as zeros (no ECC error).
- **Huge pages and memory:** 256 pages of 2 MiB reserved at boot (this run's `init.log`: `huge=256/256`); MemTotal 1,833,912 kB including that pool. No command needed more than 128 pages at once.
- **PMU:** `armv9_c1_nano`, 20 event counters plus the cycle counter. His group (cycles and raw 0x03, 0x17, 0xA2) counts user space only. 0xA2 (L3 read refill) counts on this core, so his `l3d_refill_rd_per_load` column is live; 0x2A is not implemented here.
- **DRAM caveat:** AP DRAM on this image is an embedded emulator memory, not LPDDR5X, so no DRAM latency or bandwidth number here is silicon-like.

## Reading the numbers

- No real-time clock: the folder name and every time the run read show 1970 (the clock starts at zero at boot; the 2026 date in the `uname` line is the kernel's build stamp). The run took place on 2026-10-31.
- The scratchpad is postponed to a next phase. This snapshot has no `/dev/asimov-spm`: the stock scratchpad driver's probe reads the scratchpad's error registers through the tied-off HRA leg and would hang, so the driver is left out and phase C was skipped (`sysinfo.txt` shows `ls: /dev/asimov-spm: No such file or directory`).
- OCM is mapped in 4 KiB pages and his DRAM buffers in 2 MiB pages, so the 1.25 MiB all-five OCM rows (320 pages) may include TLB misses the DRAM rows don't.
- His 256 KiB flushed walks (the DRAM 256 KiB row and all five per-region OCM rows) are not one home-node read per load. They count 1.14-1.34 L2 refills and 0.77-0.91 L3 read refills per load, and get faster as the L3 count falls (DRAM 256 KiB: 44.7, 39.0 and 35.7 ns at 0.90, 0.85 and 0.78), so some lines reach the cluster before their load, most likely by hardware prefetch, and these rows understate a single read. From 1 MiB up (the DRAM flushed rows, the OCM all-five flushed row) both counts are 1.00 per load. Part of the gap between the per-region and all-five OCM rows comes from this, not only from TLB misses.
- Three kinds of OCM row are timed without a flush first, by his commands (`run_asimov.sh` lines 113, 126, 128 and 147), though his README says every timed OCM pass starts flushed: the all-five fresh-chain latency, the four-pass all-five read, and the write rows (flushed after, inside the timed region).
- His flushed DRAM walks of 1 to 8 MiB read 52.7 ns per load, the same as his 32 MiB fresh chains (52.3 ns), so `DC CIVAC` took those lines out of the SLC as well.
- The bandwidth program's main thread shares CPU 2 with its first worker; the threads start a rep within microseconds of one another.
- SLC and DRAM numbers carry the older firmware's mesh setup.

## Cross-check against earlier pre-silicon measurements

asimov-bench measured load latencies on an older RTL of the same build kind (`hemi_rtws_tlm`, RTL `6da3026`; asimov-bench `presilicon-perf/asimov-latency/OPS.md`, the `cmn.load` notes). The CMN and C1 logic is unchanged between that RTL and this one; the DRAM model, the firmware and the methods differ. Its boots ran SCP `4148207`, which is 0.34.0 plus the HN-S-to-SMS route overrides and HN-S queue caps that this run's 0.34.0 lacks, and their DRAM was a host model. His numbers are unchanged; the gaps are noted, not corrected.
