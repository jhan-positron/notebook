Why the new read loop gains so little at 8 MiB

Easy-read version of the full report [Ref1]. Built 2026-11-15 by easy_report.py from final8.json [Ref2].

Short version

  • The question. The new read loop is 27 % faster than the old loop at a 1 MiB buffer, and only about 1 % faster at 8 MiB (first measurement: 0.94 %).
  • The cause. At 8 MiB about 83 % of the 64-byte lines come from the shared cache (SLC), and each of them costs the new loop about 0.66 extra processor cycles, which removes most of its advantage.
  • The first measurement. At 8 MiB the new loop runs at one of two rate levels, and the first measurement was at the low level.

1. The goal

The observation. The benchmark got a new read loop that only loads the data. The old loop loads the data and adds it up. The first measurement compared the two [Ref3]:

Table 1. The first measurement (2026-11-13, original program, CPUs 0 and 2).
Buffer sizeold read, GB/snew read, GB/sgain, % (calc.)
1 MiB67.1385.2627.01
8 MiB67.0867.710.94

Each rate is the best of the 12 repetitions of 0.5 s per loop and size. Gain = 100 x (new rate / old rate - 1), computed here.

The question. Why does the new loop raise the read rate by 27 % at 1 MiB, and by only 0.94 % at 8 MiB?

The machine in three lines.

  • The test machine is called CRB. Each core has two caches of its own: L1 (64 KiB) and L2 (2 MiB).
  • All cores share a third cache, the SLC (system-level cache, 128 MiB).
  • A 1 MiB buffer fits in L2. An 8 MiB buffer does not. At 8 MiB about 83 % of the 64-byte lines enter L2 from the SLC (measured, section 2) [Ref4].

What counts as an answer. Three things must hold [Ref5]:

  1. A named mechanism explains the small gain at 8 MiB.
  2. A controlled test changes that mechanism. The gain then changes as predicted, on both CPUs and in both sessions.
  3. The report states which share is explained and what stays unexplained.

2. The answer in one picture

The gain at 8 MiB is not one number. At 8 MiB the new loop runs at one of two rates, called the low and the high level (step 5). Runs at the low level show a gain of 0.2 to 3.4 %. Runs at the high level show 5.7 to 6.8 %. Each step below reports the gain of its own runs, so the values differ from step to step.

The answer in words:

  • Old read costs the same at 1 MiB and at 8 MiB: 2.67 cycles per line in all 4 cells. Between 2 and 3 MiB it costs up to 0.15 cycles per line more in some cells. The recorded check of step ZS shows this: 7 of its 36 old-read points lie more than 0.02 cycles per line away from 2.671. This rise is not explained. It does not occur at 1 MiB or at 8 MiB [Ref9].
  • The new loop is fast while the data comes from L2. At 1 MiB r is 0.00 and the loop costs 2.10 cycles per line [Ref9].
  • Each line from the SLC costs the new loop about 0.66 cycles more. A straight line fitted to the points gives about 2.08 + 0.66 x r cycles per line (medians of the 4 cells) [Ref2].
  • At 8 MiB most lines come from the SLC. There r is 0.82 to 0.83. The new loop then costs 2.51 to 2.60 cycles per line, and old read costs 2.67. So the gain is small [Ref9].
  • Nothing special happens at exactly 8 MiB. The median cost of the new loop passes the cost of old read between 12 and 16 MiB [Ref9].

Step 2. What limits the new loop at 8 MiB? Two ideas that were tested and not confirmed

Each idea had a rule in the decisions file before its data [Ref6] [Ref2].

  • H1b: the count of L1 misses is the limit.
    • Test: load-only loops with software prefetch, an instruction that asks for a line ahead of its load (steps YC and YF).
    • As recorded: "not confirmed".
    • In the one cell where a prefetch loop halved the misses, that loop was 7.99 GB/s slower than asmread, not faster. The other cells gave "Insufficient data".
  • H1d: dirty lines cost extra. At 8 MiB each line that enters L2 pushes one line back to the SLC (a write-back). The idea: a clean line would need no data in its write-back.
    • Test: an evict step that should make the buffer lines clean before the trial (steps ZC and ZD).
    • As recorded: "Insufficient data: H1d stays open".
    • The write-backs stayed at 0.82 per line. So the test did not change what it should change, and H1d is not decided.

Step 4. A controlled test: change the core rate of the loop (step ZN)

From step 3 to step 4. Step 3 measured the extra cost on a fast loop. Hypothesis H1 ("two limits") reads it this way: a loop runs at the smaller of two rates. One is its own core rate. The other is the rate at which lines arrive. H1c of step 3 names what lowers the second rate at 8 MiB: the lines come from the SLC. If H1 is right, a loop that is slow for another reason loses nothing at 8 MiB [Ref6].

Two loops that do not fit H1 in this simple form (not used as proof):

That is above the 66 GB/s threshold, so the rule does not apply to it (as recorded: "not a slow kernel (C_k above 66 GB/s): the contradiction rule does not apply").   • H1 does not hold for every loop. It is contradicted for read_nop16 in step XN.
  • Old read behaves like the slowed loops. It runs at 66.6 to 66.7 GB/s at 32 KiB and at 67.06 to 67.08 GB/s at 8 MiB (step S2). At 1 MiB it is 0.4 to 0.5 GB/s faster than at 32 KiB.

  • At 8 MiB asmread runs at 67.2 to 71.6 GB/s. That is just above old read, so the gain is small.

4. Conclusion

The three conditions of the goal, one by one. The status words follow the recorded verdicts. No recorded rule decides the three conditions themselves, so each status is the assessment of this page. The design file says: a hypothesis that is only not rejected does not make the problem solved [Ref13].

  • supported, not proven 1. A named mechanism.
    • A line that comes from the SLC costs the new loop about 0.66 cycles more. At 8 MiB r is 0.82 to 0.83, so the cost of the new loop almost reaches the cost of old read.
    • Step S2, as recorded: "P1 meets its equivalence margin; P2 read confirmed on both CPUs: True; P2 asmread confirmed on both CPUs: True; candidate supported".
    • Step ZS, as recorded: "H1c in its simple form is not contradicted".
    • Hypothesis H1 (not contradicted in step ZN, contradicted for read_nop16 in step XN): old read is limited by its own work in the core, so the extra cost does not show in its rate [Ref2].
  • partly 2. A controlled test that changes the mechanism.
    • Limit: step ZN changed the core rate of the loop. It did not change the cost of a line from the SLC.
    • Recorded: the slowed loops kept their rate at 8 MiB in 4 of 4 cells each. "H1 not contradicted by this kernel (loses more than 1.0 GB/s in 0 of 4 cells)".
    • Supplementary (defined after the data was seen): the gain of asmread over each loop was inside the predicted range in 12 of 12 cells.
    • Not done: a test that changes the cost of an SLC line itself. The one attempt (H1d, dirty lines) gave "Insufficient data" [Ref2].
  • stated 3. The share that is explained, and what is not.
    • The new loop carries 100.1 to 100.3 % of the advantage that is lost from 1 MiB to 8 MiB (step S2, recorded). The share is above 100 % because old read also lost 0.02 to 0.05 GB/s.
    • The cycle counts of step ZS give a gain of 27.0 to 27.1 % at 1 MiB and 2.8 to 6.6 % at 8 MiB. This page computed these two values. No rule fixed them before the data.
    • The full report gives the share per CPU, session and level in its table 20 [Ref1].
    • The open points are listed below.

Not explained (Insufficient data)

Each point names the measurement that would decide it. Root access was not allowed on CRB for this work [Ref4].

  • Why a line from the SLC costs about 0.66 cycles more. Needed: counters of the SLC and of the mesh (the network between the cores and the SLC), per line read. Needs root.
  • Which hardware part switches the new loop between the two levels at 8 MiB. This includes why CPU 0 was low on 2026-11-13 and high on 2026-11-15. Needed: the physical page addresses of the buffer and of the runner's record block, then mesh counters by level. Needs root. H7 (the record address) stays a hypothesis.
  • Why the rate at which lines arrive at 8 MiB differs between loops (read_nop64 and read_nop16, step 4). The sources name no deciding measurement for this point.
  • Why old read needs 2.67 cycles per line. Needed: counters that split the cycles of the old loop by pipeline stage (root). Without root: a variant of the old loop with fewer vector adds. That test was not run [Ref1].
  • Why old read costs more between 2 and 3 MiB (section 2). The sources name no deciding measurement for this point.

How the tests ran

  • Machine CRB, CPUs 2 and 41 (step H2 also CPU 0), one process at a time, no root.
  • Each proving step (S2, ZS, ZN, H2) ran in 2 sessions, 3.32 to 4.79 h apart.
  • System load at launch: 0.00 to 1.23 over all result folders. This is the 1-minute load average. About 1.00 is the test itself (est.).
  • 2,946 processes ran, and 0 were killed, failed or incomplete [Ref2] [Ref4].
