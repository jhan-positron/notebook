Why oldread stays near 67 GB/s

Short version

The old read loop processes about 67 GB/s at both 1 MiB and 8 MiB. Its L2-refill fraction rises from nearly zero to 80–82% across those sizes. The faster loop reaches about 85 GB/s at 1 MiB, then falls to 69–71.5 GB/s at 8 MiB. [Ref1]

Words used here
oldread
The old read loop in the report.
asmread
The new load-only read loop in the report.
L2
The private second-level cache of each processor core. Its capacity is 2 MiB on this machine.
SLC
The system-level cache shared by the cores. Its capacity is 128 MiB on this machine.
L2-refill fraction
The counted L2 refills divided by the number of 64-byte lines processed. The tables show this ratio as a percentage.
Throughput
Bytes processed by the loop divided by elapsed time. GB/s uses 1,000,000,000 bytes per second.
MiB
1,048,576 bytes. The original table labels these buffer sizes with M.
Cycles/64 B
Counted processor cycles per 64-byte line processed.
ZS
The report’s test step covering 13 buffer sizes on two processor cores in two sessions.
CPU and session
CPU 2 and CPU 41 identify the tested processor cores. Sessions 1 and 2 are separate runs of the test step.
Median
The middle value after the measurements are sorted. Each point summarizes 20 counted windows within one process.

Definitions follow the original report. [Ref2]

From 1 MiB to 8 MiB, the blue series falls. The orange series stays near 67 GB/s even though its L2-refill fraction rises above 80%. [Ref1]

What stays similar

  • At 1 MiB, oldread reaches 67.08–67.11 GB/s.
  • At 8 MiB, oldread reaches 66.98–67.10 GB/s.
  • At 8 MiB, its L2-refill fraction is 80.06–81.90%.

Ranges cover both cores and both sessions. [Ref1]

What changes

  • At 1 MiB, asmread reaches 85.23–85.24 GB/s.
  • At 8 MiB, asmread reaches 69.00–71.50 GB/s.
  • At 32 MiB, oldread falls to 63.89–66.13 GB/s.

The old loop is not perfectly flat over the whole size range. [Ref1]

Explanation hypothesis

The old loop’s instructions may limit its rate near 67 GB/s. Under that hypothesis, both cache paths can supply data at the rate that this loop consumes it. The faster loop exposes more of the cost of fetching data beyond L2. This is the report’s core-loop hypothesis. [Ref2]

Insufficient data: these size-sweep measurements do not identify the exact limiting hardware resource. Controlled instruction changes and core execution counters would test that part of the explanation.

How to read the cache counter

  • The rate is the loop’s throughput. It is not a direct measurement of the SLC bus bandwidth.
  • The refill percentage is a count per 64-byte line processed. It is not a direct measurement of load latency.
  • Cache sourcing changes gradually across the measured sizes. The exact counter values for both loops appear below.
  • The original Table 1 displays the new loop’s refill fraction. This page also shows the old loop’s fraction from the same analysis data. [Ref1, Ref2]
