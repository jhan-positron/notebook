Why the new read loop gains so little at 8 MiB on CRB

  • Report date 2026-11-15.
  • Generated by report_final.py from final8.json [Ref1] [Ref2].
  • System load of CRB (the test machine) at the launch of the test processes: 0.00 to 1.23. This is the 1 minute load average over all steps (table 23).

Short version

On the CRB machine the new load-only read loop is 27 % faster than the old read loop at 1 MiB. At 8 MiB it is only 0.2 to 6.7 % faster. Hypothesis H1c (each line from the shared cache costs about 0.66 extra cycles) is judged by its recorded rule in section 5.

Detail behind the Short version.

  • Cycle counts. The counted cost in processor cycles for each 64-byte line agrees with these gains.
  • Old loop. It needs 2.67 to 2.82 cycles for each line at every size from 1 to 16 MiB.
  • New loop. It needs 2.10 cycles for each line at 1 MiB. It needs 2.50 to 2.67 cycles at 8 MiB.
  • Shared cache. At 8 MiB, 83 % of the lines of the new loop come from the shared cache.
  • Cost rule. Hypothesis H1c is the cost rule behind these numbers.
  • Open questions. Root access is needed to answer two questions.
    • Why does a line from the shared cache cost more?
    • Which hardware part moves the new loop between two rates at 8 MiB?

2. The answer in one figure

  • The test. Step ZS counted processor cycles and second-level cache refills. It did so at 13 buffer sizes from 1 MiB to 32 MiB [Ref3].
  • Old read. It costs 2.669 to 2.817 cycles per line at every size from 1 to 16 MiB.
  • Check of old read. The rule of the step checks the old read points with r below 0.83 against 2.671 cycles. Of these 36 points, 29 lie within 0.02 cycles of that value (table 9).
  • Reading (hypothesis H1). Old read is limited by its own instructions inside the core. It is not limited by the caches.
    • Section 4 tests this idea on load-only loops with added instructions.
    • Section 4 also compares old read with a kernel that has the loads of old read and no adds. That comparison was defined after the data was seen.
  • New loop. It costs more cycles at the sizes where more lines come from the SLC.
  • Fitted lines. Each fitted line gives the cycles per line of the new loop in one CPU and session.
    • CPU 2 session 1: 2.089 + 0.658 × r
    • CPU 2 session 2: 2.083 + 0.667 × r
    • CPU 41 session 1: 2.079 + 0.646 × r
    • CPU 41 session 2: 2.074 + 0.663 × r
  • A fitted line describes the points of the step. It is not an independent prediction [Ref1].
  • At 8 MiB. The costs in this step are as follows.
    • New loop:
      • CPU 2 session 1: 2.519 cycles per line.
      • CPU 2 session 2: 2.525 cycles per line.
      • CPU 41 session 1: 2.505 cycles per line.
      • CPU 41 session 2: 2.596 cycles per line.
    • Old read:
      • CPU 2 session 1: 2.672 cycles per line.
      • CPU 2 session 2: 2.674 cycles per line.
      • CPU 41 session 1: 2.670 cycles per line.
      • CPU 41 session 2: 2.669 cycles per line.
  • Cycles and rate. Cycles per line and rate are two forms of one measurement. The formula is: rate = 2.8 GHz × 64 B / cycles per line.
  • So a small gap in cycles is a small gain.
  • Where the cost curves cross. Up to one size the new loop costs fewer cycles than old read. At the next size of the step it costs more.
    • CPU 2 session 1: fewer cycles at 12 MiB, more cycles at 16 MiB.
    • CPU 2 session 2: fewer cycles at 8 MiB, more cycles at 12 MiB.
    • CPU 41 session 1: fewer cycles at 12 MiB, more cycles at 16 MiB.
    • CPU 41 session 2: fewer cycles at 12 MiB, more cycles at 16 MiB.
  • These are the largest size with the lower cost and the next size of the step.
  • So 8 MiB is not a special size. It lies below the size where the two cost curves cross.

4. Test 1: change the core cost of the loop

  • The test adds no-operation instructions (instructions that do nothing) to the load-only loop. The kernels:
    • read_nop16 has 16 of them.
    • read_nop128 has 128.
    • read_sparse is a further variant of the same loop.
  • Ck. Each kernel has its own rate when the data is in the first-level cache. This rate is called Ck [Ref7].
  • Hypothesis H1 says: two limits exist, and the smaller one holds.
  • So at 8 MiB a kernel runs at the smaller of Ck and the supply rate S. S is the rate that asmread shows at 8 MiB.
  • Steps XN and ZN ran the kernels with 6 processes per cell [Ref8] [Ref9].

Supplementary: a test that changes the old loop (old read without its vector adds)

Supplementary evaluation, defined after the data was seen: old read of step S2 against read_sparse of step XN. It stands beside the recorded verdicts of this section (tables 7 and 8) and replaces none of them [Ref1].

  • The loop of old read has 16 instructions per 128 B:
    • 4 load instructions,
    • 8 vector adds,
    • 4 instructions for the counter, the pointer and the branch.
  • The loop of read_sparse also has 16 instructions per 128 B:
    • the same 4 load instructions,
    • 9 no-operation instructions,
    • 3 instructions for the pointer and the branch.
  • So read_sparse is the old loop with its 8 vector adds and its counter add replaced by no-operation instructions [Ref10].
  • At 32 KiB the data is in the first-level cache. The values there, in the 4 cells of CPU and session (table 6a):
    • old read runs at 66.62 to 66.74 GB/s,
    • read_sparse runs at 133.05 to 133.07 GB/s,
    • the ratio is 1.994 to 1.998.

8.1 Which share of the gap the cycle model explains

  • The cycle model:
    • Cycles per line of the new loop = fitted line of step ZS at the measured r.
    • Cycles per line of old read = its measured value.
    • Predicted gain = old cycles / new cycles - 1.
  • The gap to explain: the gain falls from its value at 1 MiB to its value at 8 MiB.
  • The share that the model explains = A / B.
    • A = gain at 1 MiB - predicted gain at 8 MiB.
    • B = gain at 1 MiB - measured gain at 8 MiB.
  • Measured gain by level = level rate of asmread in step S4 / old read rate in step S4 - 1.

The repeatability rule

  • The rule was fixed in the design before the data.
  • Spread of a cell. For each cell, trials 1 and 3 of a process are compared with trials 2 and 4 of the same process. The largest such difference over the 6 processes is the spread of the cell.
  • "Repeatable". A difference between two rates is "repeatable" on a CPU in a session only if both conditions hold.
    • Its median over the 6 units exceeds the sum of the two spreads.
    • The 6 unit values of the difference all have the same sign.
    • A unit is one set in step S2. In all other steps a unit is one process.
  • "Confirmed". The difference is "confirmed" only if this holds with the same sign in both sessions on both CPUs.
  • The rule is not a confidence interval [Ref26].

Deviations, as recorded in the decisions file

  • Extra logins during runs.
    • 2 extra logins fetched the data of steps XW, XCA, XCB and XCC while later steps of the same visit ran.
    • 3 extra logins did the same for steps YL, YP and YC.
    • 1 extra login did the same for step ZC.
    • Each login ran one tar command.
    • A login can disturb a running process.
  • A file outside the allowed folder. The build login of bwx10 wrote one file /tmp/.x outside the folder ~/cache-perf on CRB. The next login removed it 1 minute later.
  • The replaced YP statistic.
    • Rule 2 of round 3 named "the first fall after window 30".
    • That fall is absent in some processes.
    • The series of all up-switches replaces it (section 7.3).
  • Counters without the three-mode check.
    • The design asks for a check before counters are interpreted.
    • The check runs each trial in three modes: counters closed, counters open and not read, and counters open and read at each window.
    • This check was not run.
    • The check used instead: the counted runs must show the two levels of the uncounted step S4 within 0.5 GB/s (section 7.2).
  • A missing control. Step YF has no kernel with the code layout of the prefetch kernels and no prefetch (read_nop4). So a layout effect is not separated from the prefetch effect.

The decisions file records each of these deviations [Ref7].
