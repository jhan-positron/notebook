# Deviations and choices: run script and analysis of Parts B and C

## Short version

This file lists every point where `run_bwx.sh`, `analyze_bwx.py` and `test_analyze_bwx.py` add to, or differ from, specification v1 of the handoff. Most points are choices where the handoff was open. Three points change what a result means and need a decision of the lead before they are used: A5 (end skew with `--stop pass`), A6 (a process with an irregular interval gives no value) and A12 (B4, reading of "all with full coverage"). Points S19, S20, A26 to A31, P1 and P2 were added after the review of 2026-11-13. Of these, A28 (B5 with a disagreement of the two metrics) and P1 (rows of A3 in the test of q) also need the lead's decision.

## Words used here

- **Handoff**: `crb/new-test-kernel/handoff.md`. Section names such as B.9 and B.10 are its sections.
- **Plan**: `crb/new-test-kernel/plan.md`.
- **`bwx`**: the changed benchmark of Parts B and C. Another agent wrote it.
- **`decide.py`**: the decision rules of B.10 as code, in `crb/new-test-kernel/`.
- **Process**: one start of `bwx`.
- **Interval**: one timed entry of a sequence.
- **Setting**: one combination of stop method, interval length and start mode, written `stop/min_ms/start`.
- **A/A spread**: the largest absolute difference between two copies of one loop, over the processes of a group.
- **M_hist, M_sum**: the two rate formulas of B.10.
- **Irregular interval**: an interval with a late thread, or with an end skew above 5 ms (B.4).
- **CRB**: the test machine.
- **est.**: an estimate, not a measurement.

## Analysis (`analyze_bwx.py`)

| No | Point of the handoff | What the analysis does | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Entry of an interval | The entry (kernel and size) of an interval comes from `--seq` in the header line and from `pos`. The kernel field of the row must match, or the process is left out and listed. | `bwx` prints the kernel name without `@SIZE`. |
| A2 | Rate of an interval | From the T rows: bytes divided by elapsed time. The rounded value of the I row is not used. | Two decimals in the I row are too coarse for an A/A spread. |
| A3 | Incomplete process | A process is used only if it has no `-- killed` or `-- failed` line, every expected interval, and as many T rows per interval as threads. Other processes are listed under "left out" with the reason. | B.10 has no rule for a part of a process. |
| A4 | Round numbers | The rounds are ordered by their printed number. `bwx` counts from 0. | The handoff does not say where rounds start. |
| A5 | End skew with `--stop pass` | An interval is irregular if a thread has the flag `late` (any stop method), or if the end skew is above 5 ms **and the process uses `--stop common`**. With `--stop pass` the end skew is not a reason. | B.4 defines the end skew inside the protocol of `common`. With `pass` the threads end at pass borders, so a skew above 5 ms is expected. The rule would otherwise remove the reproduction condition of B7. This needs the lead's decision. |
| A6 | Irregular interval inside a process | The process gives no value D, E or spread for a quantity that needs the irregular interval. The interval is listed. The setting can then fall below 4 processes and become "not observed". | B.10 says that an irregular interval is not used. It does not say how to form a mean over rounds without it. The strict reading adapts nothing. |
| A7 | M_hist against M_sum | The same decision is made with both. If the word or the sign differs, the result is "not resolved", and both are in the output. The handoff names only different "words". A different sign is treated the same way. The same check is applied to a change of D between two settings. | A resolved D of opposite sign is a stronger disagreement than a different word. |
| A8 | B7 with one kernel per process | Coded in `single_kernel()` of the analysis, as B.10 words it. The rule is applied with M_hist and with M_sum. If the word or the sign differs, the result is "not resolved", and both values are in the output (see A27). | `decide.py` has no function for this rule. |
| A9 | B9 | Pattern: "same pattern" if every regular interval of both kinds has one label, split or uniform. "Pattern as H9a predicts" if one buffer is split and two buffers are uniform. Otherwise "not resolved". Total: median of the process values with one buffer minus that with two. The A/A value is the largest difference between two processes of one kind. Resolved if the difference is above it and the two sets do not overlap. | B.9 gives only "the A/A value is the difference between two processes of one kind". `decide.py` has no function. |
| A10 | B5 | A variant "shows the pattern and total of old read" if its label equals that of old read in every process, and `decide_setting` on (old read minus variant, asm read 2 minus asm read) gives "not resolved". With fewer than 4 processes with a value: "not observed". | Direct reading, with the count of B7. |
| A11 | Percentiles of q | Nearest rank: the value at rank ceil(p x n), counted from 1. With 120 threads these are the 12th and the 108th smallest rates. | The handoff names no method. |
| A12 | B4, cases (a) and (b) | Per CPU and session, over all used processes of the CPU. Case (a): at least 10 low intervals with full coverage and at least 10 high intervals with full coverage. Case (b): the same with coverage none, in any process, not only with `--no-thp`. Intervals with `NA` or partial coverage count for no case. | "At least 10 low intervals and at least 10 high intervals, all with full coverage" cannot mean all intervals of the CPU, because half of the processes run with `--no-thp`. This needs the lead's check. |
| A13 | B4 over two sessions | H3a is "rejected" if one CPU shows a case in every session. The case may differ between the sessions. | "Occurs on one CPU in both sessions" does not say whether the case must be the same. |
| A14 | B4, level change | Also counted: changes between low and high from one labelled interval to the next, and how many of them have equal coverage and equal `thp_collapse_alloc` and `pgmigrate_success` before both intervals. | The plan (B4) asks for "no sign of a page change". It is a description, not a decision. |
| A15 | B6, A/A spread of a CPU | The largest absolute A/A value over all complete processes of the CPU, also those whose references change level. | "Over its processes". This can only make the spread larger. |
| A16 | B3 | The change is decided with `decide_change(..., min_n=3)` per CPU and label, between B1 at 8 MiB of the same session and the build. Without B1 in the same session: "not observed". For H6 over the sessions: "not observed" if a build is missing or has no CPU with 3 processes of one label in B1 and in the build. | B.10 names no smallest count for B3. 3 is the count of B1. Without the "not observed" case, a session with no data would count as "resolved on no CPU". |
| A17 | B8 | A count has the label of its regular intervals of both processes. A bracket is built only if all processes have the same count of mesh-0 threads. H8 "supported": the upper end of the bracket of asm read is at or below the lower end of the bracket of old read. | "Wholly below" is read as: the brackets may share one end point. |
| A18 | B10 | Controls and H11 with `controls_from_aa` and `decide_h11`. The change of the spread with `decide_change`, with D = spread of the kernel and E = spread of asm write 2 minus spread of asm write. The change of the median total is a description without a decision. B10 uses M_hist only. M_sum of the median total is in the output. | B.10 gives no rule for the change of the median total. |
| A19 | Words over two sessions | `two_sessions()` gives "rejected" only if the bounded prediction failed in every session, and at least 2 sessions are in the input. With one session the word is "not rejected (one session only ...)". For B8 and H11 the plan's word is added in brackets. The mapping from a step to a hypothesis (H1, H3a, H4, H5, H6, H7a, H7b, H8, H9a, H11, the B5 statement) is my reading of the plan. Each entry keeps the result of each session and one line that says what "fail" means. | The handoff asks for one of the five words for each hypothesis in `results.md`. The lead writes that file. These entries are a help, not the final word. |
| A20 | H5 | Only the variant `asmcopy_c` is mapped to H5. "Fail" also when it is resolved with the sign opposite to B1. "Not observed" when B1 of the session has no resolved sign. The two other variants are reported as groups without a hypothesis word. | The plan states the prediction for the C instruction order only. |
| A21 | H11 over two sessions | The analysis does not check that the same mesh rose in both sessions. The values high minus low per mesh are in the output for each process. | `decide_h11` does not return the mesh, and the rule is not coded a second time. |
| A22 | Part C | Description only: per group the median of the rate, of cycles per CPU nanosecond (GHz), and of each count per 64 B of the bytes of the T row. A C row with `NA` times, or with running time below enabled time, is not used. One-thread asm read intervals at 8 MiB get the level of their own rate. Other intervals get no level. | The handoff gives no decision rule for Part C. C2 intervals are not labelled by their reference intervals. |
| A23 | Page state | B4 and Part C use the P row of the same round and position (one-thread processes). For processes with more than one thread the P rows are parsed and counted (`NA` rows per step), and no page state is assigned to an interval. | No rule of B.10 uses the page state of a multi-thread process. |
| A24 | Common-time rate | Not computed. The output states the limit. | B.10 forbids a common-time rate from the totals. |
| A25 | SHORT | No decision. One line per process: complete or not, number of intervals, P rows with `NA`, C rows, late intervals, largest end skew. | The short run checks the output of `bwx`. |
| A26 | H7a and H7b for read (B7), over the sessions | A session gives "not observed" for H7a and H7b if its reproduction condition (`pass/500/common`) has no resolved D of the sign of 2026-11-13 (old read above new read), neither with the kernels in one process nor with one kernel per process. Before, such a session gave "fail", and two such sessions gave the word "rejected". The new field is `reproduced_in_either_way`. | Plan, step B7: without the reproduction "no cause is assigned". "No factor changed D" is no failed prediction when there is no D. |
| A27 | B7 with one kernel per process, M_hist against M_sum | `single_kernel()` decides with both metrics, as `both_metrics()` does for a setting. Before, it used M_hist only. | B.10 names M_hist as the decision metric "for the reproduction condition and for every setting", with "not resolved" when M_sum gives another word. With `--stop pass` the threads end at pass borders, so the two metrics can differ in real data. B9 still uses M_hist only for its total (A9): B.10 gives no rule for B9. |
| A28 | B5, when M_hist and M_sum disagree on the total difference | The variant gets "not resolved", never "yes", and the note keeps the text of the disagreement. "Yes" needs the same pattern and "not resolved" under both metrics. Over the sessions such a session counts as "not resolved", so the B5 statement cannot become "rejected" through it. Before, the disagreement was turned into "not resolved" for the total, and that was read as "shows the total of old read". | This departs from the letter of B.10 ("call the result not resolved" and, for B5, "is not resolved against the A/A spread"). | A29 | H7a and H7b for read, a change of D on which the two metrics disagree | If no change of D is resolved in a session and at least one pair of settings has such a disagreement, the session counts as "not resolved", not as "fail". | Same reason as A28. ## What the tests cover

`test_analyze_bwx.py` has 28 tests on synthetic files in the format of B.3. 6 of them were added after the review of 2026-11-13, for A26 to A31. 2 more were added on 2026-11-14, for A32 and for A33. Each of the 6 fails on the analysis as it was before the review, and the 20 earlier tests pass on both versions. The rest of this paragraph is about the first 20. Each test states why its rule matters. I also changed the analysis in 16 ways, one at a time, and ran the tests on each changed copy. 15 changes made a test fail. The one change that no test saw removed the check that two reference intervals are 3 positions apart. In the sequences of B.9 that check never decides anything.
