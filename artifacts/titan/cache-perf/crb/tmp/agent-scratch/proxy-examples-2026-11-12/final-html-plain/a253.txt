<p><b>Method.</b></p><ul><li>One core (the builder) builds a chain through a buffer and walks it once.</li><li>After that walk, the lines are expected to be in the builder's chiplet. Most of them are expected in the slices of that chiplet. The last 2 MiB of the walk are expected in the builder's L2. This run did not count where the lines are.</li><li>A second core (the walker) then walks two new chains through the same lines, one after the other. The time of each walk is recorded.</li><li>Pairs across chiplets, builder to walker: CPU 3 to 67, 67 to 3, 64 to 3, and 3 to 119.</li><li>Controls, where one core builds and walks: CPU 3, and CPU 67. No pair of two cores on one chiplet was run.</li><li>Each pair ran once at each size [results/followup2-20261031T073513Z/cross.csv].</li></ul><div class="tw"><table><tr><th>Buffer</th><th>One core builds and walks, both walks, ns per load</th><th>Walker on the other chiplet, first walk, ns per load</th><th>Walker on the other chiplet, second walk, ns per load</th></tr><tr><td>16 MiB</td><td>29.0 to 29.3</td><td>85.0 to 88.5</td><td>31.7 to 33.2</td></tr><tr><td>32 MiB</td><td>30.2 to 30.3</td><td>82.8 to 87.7</td><td>32.7 to 37.3</td></tr><tr><td>64 MiB</td><td>48.7 to 51.5</td><td>93.6 to 97.9</td><td>51.9 to 58.2</td></tr></table></div><p><b>What the numbers show.</b></p><ul><li><b>First walk.</b> At 16 MiB the first walk from the other chiplet costs 56 to 59 ns more for each load than the walks of the controls.</li><li><b>Hypothesis for the first walk.</b> The walker's mesh does not hold the line at the first walk. The request goes to the builder's chiplet, and the data returns over the chip-to-chip links. No counter traced the path. A run that counts the accesses on both meshes during the first walk would test this.</li><li><b>Second walk.</b> At 16 MiB the second walk takes 31.7 to 33.2 ns for each load. That is close to SLC latency on the walker's own mesh.</li><li><b>Hypothesis for the second walk.</b> After the first walk the lines are in the slices of the walker's mesh (section "Lines homed on the other mesh are served by a local proxy"). This run did not count where they are. A count of SLC accesses on each mesh during the second walk would test this.</li><li><b>Remainder.</b> The second walk is 2.6 to 4.1 ns for each load slower than the same walker measures alone on a 16 MiB buffer in another run. That value is 28.9 to 29.1 ns for each load for the walkers, CPUs 3, 67 and 119 [results/followup3-20261102T031936Z/percore-latency-1.csv]. This difference is not explained.</li><li><b>64 MiB.</b> All walks are slower at 64 MiB, also the walks of the controls. Another run counted SLC misses for 11 % of the loads at 64 MiB (section "Effective capacity is 64 MiB for one buffer"). Misses of that kind would explain the slower walks. This run did not count them.</li><li><b>Bill's number.</b> The first walk of 85 to 88 ns for each load agrees with the 81 to 88 ns that Bill reports for "L3, other domain" (Bill's label).</li></ul><p><b>Caveats.</b></p><ul><li><b>Lines in the builder's L2.</b> At 16 MiB about one eighth of the lines (the last 2 MiB that the builder walked) may be in the builder's L2 at the first walk. The builder's L2 would then send those lines to the walker, and no slice would be read. The test does not separate them.</li><li><b>Modified lines only.</b> The builder writes every line when it builds the chains. So the test covers lines that the builder modified. It does not cover lines that were only read.</li><li><b>Two home cases.</b> By the mesh rule (section "Lines homed on the other mesh are served by a local proxy"), half of the lines are homed on the walker's mesh and half on the builder's mesh. The homes of these buffers were not measured. The test does not separate the two cases.</li></ul>
