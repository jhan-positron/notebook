<p class="take"><b>So all of a core's cached lines live on its own mesh, as home lines and as proxy lines in the same 64 slices. That is why one buffer sees 64 MiB of SLC: one mesh's 64 slices of 1 MiB.</b> The earlier explanation, that a buffer is homed on one mesh, was wrong and is withdrawn. Home lines and proxy lines share each slice's storage, and firmware can cap the share of each kind with an occupancy limit that resets to the maximum (CMN S3 TRM, capacity partitioning, pages 335 to 336, and the register fields on page 1039). The limits were not read. A buffer whose lines are homed half and half fills a slice half and half by demand, whatever the limits allow. So the 64 MiB figure is measured, and the only inference about the limits is that neither was below about half for this workload (est.).</p>
