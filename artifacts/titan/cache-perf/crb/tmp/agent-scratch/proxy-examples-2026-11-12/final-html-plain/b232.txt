<p>The home node of a line is fixed by the address hash, and the hash spreads every 2 MiB chunk evenly: each of the 16 chunks put exactly 16,384 of its 32,768 lines on each mesh [<code>full/chunks.csv</code>]. The hash is per line: within every chunk the mesh is the XOR of address bits 6, 8, 10, ... 20 (the pattern of the first 16 lines is 0101 1010 0101 1010, and 80 % of neighbouring lines have different homes), and 4 of the 16 chunks come out inverted, so higher physical-address bits, which user space cannot see, also enter the hash [<code>full/homes.csv</code>, checked on 2026-11-02 over all 524,288 lines]. So half of the lines of any buffer of 2 MiB or more are homed on the other mesh, and a small buffer need not split evenly. A core still sees those lines at 19 to 44 ns, the same range as its own mesh, and the latency to a remote slice is anti-correlated with the latency to the same-numbered local slice (correlation -0.74, median over cores). Arm's design explains it: a home node also acts as a proxy for addresses homed on the other mesh (the LCN role of the super home node, whose proxy lines share the slice with its home lines as the LCC) [CMN S3 TRM, Super Home Node, HBT and LBT, page 55]. The counters confirm the service: in the cache-state check, the 4 remote slices saw 0.00 to 0.01 accesses per load at their home while the loads were served at SLC latency, and during a repeated 8 MiB read stream from CPU 0 the other mesh counted nothing at all (0.000 accesses per line on arm_cmn_1 against 0.827 on arm_cmn_0) [<code>writebw.csv</code>, case asmread-8M-1, analysed with <code>bench/subtract.py</code>]. Hypothesis: which proxy serves which home is a fixed function of the home's position, roughly its mirror image, which would fit two identical chiplets placed rotated. The anti-correlation is the only evidence for it, and this report does not use the remote rows to place anything.</p>
