Findings

  • Pair port: cores 2k and 2k+1 share one crosspoint port. Two partners together read 78 GB/s from the mesh cache; two non-partners 134 GB/s.
  • One writer's 73 GB/s limit is inside the core: no L1/L2 allocation (0 refills, 0 write-backs), no credit stalls on its crosspoint links, and the same port carries 82 GB/s with the partner core, 229 GB/s with 4 writers [sudo-20261102T211150Z/writepath.csv].
  • Slice distance: nearest slice 18.8 to 22.2 ns, 2.7 ns per crosspoint hop, farthest 32.9 to 44.2 ns. Repeat spread 3 %.
  • Every core's position is known from its latency gradient (map below): CPUs 0 to 63 on mesh 0, 64 to 119 on mesh 1, numbered column by column; pairs 34/35 and 98/99 sit on a crosspoint without slices.
  • Every 2 MiB chunk of memory is split half and half between the two meshes by an address hash (the XOR of address bits 6, 8, ... 20 and higher bits), not line by line. A hit is always served on the core's own mesh, by the home slice or by a proxy slice for lines homed on the other mesh, at the same 19 to 44 ns. A line held only on the other mesh costs about 85 ns on its first access, then it is kept locally. So one core's data lives on its own mesh: one buffer sees 64 MiB, not 128.
  • Streaming writes largely bypass the mesh cache: 0.013 fills and 0.76 memory requests per line at 8 MiB per thread. The 73 GB/s "mesh write" is mostly the DRAM write path.
  • DRAM write collapses only with heavy writers on both meshes: 64 writers on one mesh 394 GB/s, 32 on each mesh 173 GB/s. Reads do not collapse. Home-node retry and throttle counts fall in the collapsed case; the bottleneck is not located (hypothesis: chip-to-chip links).
  • Concurrent reads from the mesh cache: 32 threads on one mesh share about 869 GB/s (25 to 30 each); 8 threads on the other mesh get 58 to 60 each (3 MiB per thread, one thread per pair, 1372 GB/s total).

Open

  • Bottleneck of the cross-mesh write collapse: chip-to-chip gateway and memory-controller counters not yet read.
  • Slice partition between home lines and proxy lines (configuration register, not perf), and the excess whole-mesh accesses and misses beyond the timed loads, recorded but not attributed.
  • Cross-chiplet first access: 85 to 88 ns per load at 16 MiB for lines the other chiplet's slices hold, 32 ns on the second walk (matches Bill's 81 to 88 ns); remote-slice hits and lines still in the builder's L2 not separated.
