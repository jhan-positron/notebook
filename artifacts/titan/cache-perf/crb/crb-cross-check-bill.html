CRB Cache Cross-Check

Our 2026-10-30 cache measurements on the Phoenix CRB compared with Bill's independent evaluation of 2026-10-01/02 (write-up dated 2026-10-19, ~/tmp/WRITEUP-phoenix-crb-evaluation.md). Written 2026-10-31.

Short version.

Bill's numbers agree with ours on every cache level and on DRAM: L1, L2, mesh cache and DRAM latency within a few percent, the same 5.5 ns repeated-chase artifact, the same read peak, the same single-chiplet read (357 versus 358 GB/s) and the same collapse when every core streams. His two-chiplet finding supports our "two 64 MiB halves" hypothesis. Two of his numbers we could not reproduce on 2026-10-31: his 81 to 88 ns for a line cached by the other chiplet (we measure 2 to 7 ns extra), and his 551 GB/s write with spread placement (we reach 447 GB/s, which is the same 70 % of peak once the DIMM count is taken into account).

Words used here

CRB
Arm's Phoenix reference server board, 128 Neoverse V3 cores at 2.8 GHz. Ours is 192.168.2.45, Bill's was 192.168.2.46 (see "Same board?").
Mesh cache (SLC)
The 128 MiB cache that Linux calls L3. Bill calls it "L3, own domain" and finds about 64 MB per chiplet.
Chiplet
Bill's term for each of the two 64-core dies, cores 0 to 63 and 64 to 127, each with its own mesh. Our counters call them arm_cmn_0 and arm_cmn_1.
Repeated chase
A pointer chase that walks the same random cycle again and again. The core learns the sequence and the result (5.5 ns) is not a cache latency.
Spread placement
Threads pinned to cores far apart on the chip, versus "adjacent" (cores 0, 1, 2, ...) or "even" (0, 2, 4, ...).

1. Latency

Takeaway: every latency agrees within a few percent except two rows. At 32 to 64 MiB our 30.5 ns is the fresh-chain value at 32 to 56 MiB and rises to 39 ns at 64 MiB, inside Bill's 35 to 49 ns. For a line cached by the other chiplet we measure 31.7 to 37.3 ns against his 81 to 88 ns; see section 6.

2. Bandwidth

Takeaway: read peak, single-chiplet read, adjacent-core write and the all-core collapse match. Spread-placement write is 447 GB/s here against his 551 GB/s, the same 70 to 72 % of each board's channel peak.

4. What Bill adds that we did not measure

  • Two chiplets with separate mesh caches. His core-to-core test (292 ns same domain, 402 ns other) and the 81 to 88 ns "other chiplet" latency locate the boundary at core 64. Our counters saw a buffer homed in one mesh but could not say why. Together: each chiplet owns 64 MiB, and a buffer is homed where it was first cached. Our report's open item on this can be marked as supported.
  • Interference. One streaming core raises mesh-cache latency for its whole chiplet from 26 to 31 ns up to 88 to 92 ns. L2 is never affected. The other chiplet barely notices. We measured only idle latency.
  • Placement. 16 adjacent cores reach 150 to 172 GB/s, the same 16 threads spread across the chip reach 350 GB/s. This explains our core-pair port finding (about 80 GB/s per pair) and our low 16-thread numbers.
  • Production spec. This board runs at 2.8 GHz instead of 3.2 to 3.5 GHz, and public spec is DDR5-8800 with "sub-100 ns" DRAM latency. All our numbers are for the pre-production board.

5. Same board?

Bill's write-up names arm@192.168.2.46 with 12 × 32 GB DIMMs and 377 GB usable. Ours is jhan@192.168.2.45, hostname ubuntu, with 10 × 32 GB DDR5-8000 DIMMs by dmidecode and 329 GB MemTotal. Either there are two boards or the DIMM population changed between 2026-10-02 and 2026-10-30. This matters for peak DRAM bandwidth: 12 channels give 768 GB/s theoretical, 10 give 640 GB/s. His 537 GB/s read would be 70 % of 768, our 523 GB/s is 82 % of 640. Insufficient data to say which; one dmidecode -t 17 on 192.168.2.46 resolves it.

6. Follow-ups, run 2026-10-31 07:35 UTC

  • Spread write: done. 16 threads 8 cores apart over both chiplets write 447 GB/s and read 411 GB/s; 30 threads 4 apart write 426 GB/s and read 533 GB/s (a new read best). 16 threads on chiplet 0 only: write 373, read 357 GB/s. Our 447 GB/s is 70 % of our 640 GB/s peak; Bill's 551 GB/s is 72 % of 768 GB/s if his board has 12 channels. The report now carries 447 and 533.
  • Cross-chiplet latency: done, and it disagrees with Bill. A chain built and walked once on core 3 and then chased from core 67 (other chiplet) costs 32.1 ns at 16 MiB and 32.7 ns at 32 MiB, against 29.0 and 30.2 ns when core 3 chases its own chain. Built on 67, chased from 3: 33.2 and 35.5 ns. Built on 3, chased from 119: 32.2 and 37.3 ns. So the penalty is 2 to 7 ns, not 50. Hypothesis: Bill's 81 to 88 ns is for lines still dirty in the other core's L2 (a cache-to-cache transfer over the chiplet link), while our lines had been evicted to the mesh cache by the 16 to 64 MiB footprint. Test: a 1 MiB set written by a core on the other chiplet and read at once. Files: results/followup2-20261031T073513Z/cross.csv and spread.csv.
  • DIMM count on 192.168.2.46: still open. Insufficient data; one dmidecode -t 17 there resolves it.
