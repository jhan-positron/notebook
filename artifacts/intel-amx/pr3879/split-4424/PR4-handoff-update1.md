## Short version

All five points and all three corrections are accepted. Two points (the P1s) are defects of the inherited whole-machine campaign script that the handoff's schedule makes relevant: its cleanup path releases Bill's marker even when the campaign never took it, and restores packages and serving without testing the CI lease. ## 2. Changes applied to PR4-handoff.md

### 2.1 Section 5.3, Campaign W: marker ownership (point 1)

A new sub-bullet "Marker ownership" tells the executor to add two flags to the fork next to RESTORED (campaign.sh:216): W_TOOK_MARKER, set to 1 right after a successful `bill take` (:398), and W_TOOK_MACHINE, set to 1 right before the first state change (the lease re-test at :429-431). restore_and_release runs the hwattn-clear, the package restore, serving-down and serving-up and the config.env check only when W_TOOK_MACHINE is 1, and calls `bill release` only when W_TOOK_MARKER is 1; release_lock stays unconditional. The acceptance test is a stubbed fixture like the chain's cleanup-test.sh (every dut.sh and bill-share.sh call a logging stub), recorded as exec/pr4ci-<date>/w-cleanup-test.log: a SIGTERM or an abort while W waits for the lease or the hand-off marker makes no bill-share.sh call and no dut.sh call; a normal completion after the take makes exactly one release; a SIGTERM after the first pass makes the full restore and exactly one release. Section 5.3's Bill's-marker bullet, section 8 (Campaign W) and section 9 now say that the flags remove the release from those paths and that the "never stop W under the chain" rule stays as a second layer; section 9 forbids launching the inherited campaign.sh unforked.

### 2.2 Section 5.3, Campaign W: the takeover path (point 2)

A new sub-bullet "The takeover path" tells the executor that restore_and_release re-tests the lease (lease_state, :313) before each mutation: before hwattn-clear, before ensure-base, before serving-down and before serving-up. When the lease is busy at any of those points, the fork makes no further dut.sh mutation and no `bill release`, writes exec/results/pr4ci-<date>/restore-deferred.txt (the restore target, the config.env state, whether the marker is still taken, the time), sets the status "TAKEOVER: restore deferred", adds RESTORE-DEFERRED to problems and still releases the flock. The executor finishes the restore by hand after the lease clears (hwattn-clear when config.env is not 0 bytes, ensure-base when the installed package is not the nightly's, serving-down and serving-up, then `bill-share.sh release`) and records each result. After a DUT outage the same re-tests run when the DUT answers again. The fixture covers a takeover between passes, a driver rc 9 and a lease that turns busy between two restore steps. Section 7's stop list and section 5.4's campaign criterion name restore-deferred.txt.

### 2.3 Section 5.3, Campaign W, and section 5.4 (point 3)

A new sub-bullet "The recovered-pass count": at "repeat ok" the fork runs `fails=$((fails-1)); recovered="$recovered $tag"`, keeps the first attempt's folder <tag>-failed1, and adds the field `recovered passes:` to outcome.txt. A successful repeat is acceptable; the body names the recovered tag with the first attempt's failure. Two failed attempts, an incomplete cell and a failed restore stay failures. Section 5.4's campaign criterion now reads: the .done marker exists, outcome.txt reads the four all-none fields plus the recovered field, and a recovered pass is reported with its <tag>-failed1 folder. Section 6's Measurements part (b) names a recovered pass. Section 8 (Campaign W) notes the inherited counter.
