## PR body (as it is now; may have been edited after the review)

Treat each gof's two 8 KB hwcacheblock staging buffers (k.data/v.data) as a reconstructable cache: free them once every in-flight DMA that reads them has completed, and rebuild on demand (reacquire + rebuild position tables + repopulate from the host KV pages) when a later submission needs the GOF. The durable copies remain the host books and device HBM, so the free is a cache eviction -- GOF identity (gal.gofs[layer], gof_job refs, shard state) is unchanged. See Note [GOF staging eviction].

Interface / behavior:
- gof: add dma_inflight (atomic) + staging_mutex, prepare_for_dma() (reacquire + populate + reserve a credit under the mutex before xfer_gof), free_staging_if_idle() (frees both buffers once dma_inflight hits 0), and the inflight_guard RAII credit (commit() after xfer_gof keeps it).
- hwcacheblock: alloc_buffer/release_buffer/clear/is_allocated; k/v gain non-virtual rebuild_positions()/reacquire_buffer() (no new pos virtuals).
- pending_xfer: add pinned_gof (shared_ptr<gof>) pinning staging for the life of its in-flight DMA and serving as the dma_inflight decrement target.
- dma_tracker::push() is now noexcept (a post-submit failure terminates rather than unwinding into a free-under-DMA); poll_completions decrements and frees outside dma_tracker::mu to avoid a staging_mutex<->mu lock-order inversion.
- Submission sites gof_rodeo_impl and backfill_shard route through prepare_for_dma / commit / push.

Tests:
- t_dma_tracker (pos_fake): staging lifecycle, inflight_guard, poll-frees-after- last, clear-drops-pin, prepare/free allocation-neutrality.
- t_gof_dma (FPGA): free -> reacquire -> fill -> xfer -> readback; and prepare_for_dma reacquire/populate/credit.
- t_gof_staging_leaks (FPGA, new): real DMA-heap allocation-balance leak suite across submit/track/complete/free + multi-device fanout.

Validated: make build-test clean; t_dma_tracker 33/1718; t_gof_dma and t_gof_staging_leaks pass on FPGA (slice-scoped); make format + lint clean.

> [!NOTE]
> **High Risk**
> Changes concurrent DMA submission, completion, and host buffer lifetime for hardware KV offload; races or premature free could corrupt in-flight transfers or leak staging memory.
> 
> **Overview**
> **GOF host staging is now evictable** after DMA lands in HBM: each `gof`'s two 8 KB `hwcacheblock` buffers are treated as a cache over the durable host KV pages and device copy, not permanent allocations.
> 
> `hwcacheblock` gains explicit **alloc/release/reacquire** and **`build_indexes()`** so K/V pointer tables stay valid when staging is freed and reallocated. `gof` adds **`dma_inflight`**, a **`staging_busy` CAS gate**, **`prepare_for_dma()`** (reacquire, populate, reserve credit), **`free_staging_if_idle()`**, and **`inflight_guard`** (rollback unless **`commit()`** after `xfer_gof`).
> 
> **`dma_tracker`** records **`pinned_gof`** on each `pending_xfer`, makes **`push()` noexcept**, and **`poll_completions()`** decrements credits and calls **`free_staging_if_idle()` outside the tracker mutex**. **`gof_rodeo_impl`** and **`backfill_shard`** use the prepare/commit/push path and hold **`shared_ptr<gof>`** through submit.
> 
> Tests cover fake and FPGA paths, including new **`t_gof_staging_leaks`** for DMA-heap balance and tracker lifecycle.

## Wade's conversation comments

### issue-comment 4674512577 at 2026-06-10T21:05
I can't help it lol... I'm always concerned with performance. So I asked Claude to summarize my concern (cuz it often words things better than I can):

> **Eviction is unconditional on every completion** — rebuild thrashing risk. free_staging_if_idle frees whenever dma_inflight reaches 0, and populate (the swizzle) is the bulk of the rebuild cost. For the prefill→HBM offload pattern this is a clear win (staging genuinely isn't needed again). But for any pattern that re-DMAs the same GOF in quick succession (multi-shard backfill fanout, speculation), you now pay a full free + reacquire + reswizzle each time, with no hysteresis.

Maybe it won't materialize, but after this lands in `main` we might find combinations where this has a larger impact than what we speculated. So just seems worth keeping in mind in case we need to optimize it a bit later.
