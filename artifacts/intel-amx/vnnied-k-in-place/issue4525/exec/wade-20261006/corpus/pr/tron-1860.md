WADE: Just for my own clarity: the way I understood our conversation on this, the goal is to (e.g.) be able to DMA-xfer a large block of data which may span _many_ descriptors, but only rely on completion/notification of the _last_ descriptor in the list.

If we look at how `xfer_k_entry_blocking()` and `xfer_v_entry_blocking()` work today, that's exactly the model they're following. They only wait on the last descriptor to complete.

I think the main difference is today, `xfer_k_entry_blocking()` and `xfer_v_entry_blocking()` assume at most **one wrap** between enqueue and completion time. But when we start sending much larger transfers, we may wrap **multiple** times. So the focus here is really about the _accuracy_ of 'done', not so much about _performance_ of that decision, right?
