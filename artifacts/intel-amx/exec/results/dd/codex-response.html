C2. A tie explains why the word changed; it does not prove the numbers are correct. Do not call AMX's differences "admissible" just because they look like other differences from the same code. The two axes are reported separately. Tie: codex's own formula, filled in from the data at step 22: margin_off = 0.0000; delta_gap = -0.125; margin_on = 0 + (-0.125) = -0.125, so the sign flips. Correctness: we did not replay the production tensors through a high-precision reference. What we have instead: (1) the kernel unit tests, which replay the same synthetic inputs through the AMX routines and an exact reference with a declared bound, passed in all three builds (6158 / 6414 / 6157 checks); (2) a non-AMX control (AMX off, two fewer cores) produces differences of the same kind and size and flips the same tie. The verdict wording was changed from "not a bug" to "no AMX defect found by these tests".A same-input replay of the exact attention call at step 22 (AVX vs AMX vs fp64 reference) would close this axis. A small, production-shape defect hiding under the control's noise level is not excluded by what we ran.

How to read the two documents together. Codex asked for a trustworthy measurement before any hypothesis is chosen. On the gates we ran, the measurement is trustworthy: same-setting runs are exact, the replay is exact, AMX demonstrably ran, and the two AMX layouts agree. The flip itself is fully explained by an exact tie. What the campaign does not do is prove the AMX numbers correct on the production tensors at the flip; it shows only that nothing in the tests distinguishes them from a harmless arithmetic change. The verdict text uses that wording.
