A count near the nightly's means the client kept up with the stream. A count far above it means the client stalled and then received bursts. Stalls lower some TPS samples and raise short TTFTs.

  • What the client load can and cannot change. TPS is a rate over a window of generated tokens late in each request (the glossary gives the window). On base, llama-3b (the config with 121 anomalous samples) came out -1.6 % against the same-day nightly. The other base configs were within 0.9 % of the nightly, except gpt-oss-120b (+5.3 %, inside its own 13-night band). A stall of 121 samples therefore moved a TPS mean by about 2 % at most. It cannot explain a 5 % change in a config with 3 anomalous samples. TTFT is one timestamp difference on the client and moves by the whole stall. TTFT comparisons that involve the base arm for llama-3b, llama-8b and qwen-3-4b tp2 (the three configs whose base TTFT differs from the same-day nightly by more than 5 %, section 2), or the target arm for configs 1 to 5, therefore cannot be attributed to the package.
