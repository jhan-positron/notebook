  • At tp2 the exposed cost is the main-thread K store (H1), and its size equals the micro-benchmark prediction. With 8 users the layout costs +0.148 ms per step under the default early launch (-1.8 % TPS, t +10.2, n = 3). Reading of night 2

  • H1 confirmed in place. The main thread's Save K grew from 4.6 to 20.3 us per layer at tp2 with 8 users and from 5.4 to 21.0 us per layer at tp4 (+15.7 us per layer, +566 us per step, serial). The main thread's LLC misses rose 2.6x at tp2 (3.05 to 8.01 million in 3 s) and 1.7x at tp4 while its L1 and L2 misses did not move: the scatter's 64 partial-line writes per row miss the core's own caches and are served by the L3, as the micro-benchmark modelled.
  • H3 is larger than the micro-benchmark predicted and is the second cause. The busiest worker's pending section (the tail scored with qk_group) grew from 337 to 742 us per pass at tp2 with 8 users (+405 us, x2.2) and from 540 to 914 us at tp4 (+374 us). Per call that is about +3.5 us against the benchmark's +0.56 us: in the live step the worker reads the 64 lines while the main thread is still storing other planes. The workers' L1 misses rose 25 % and their L2 misses 40 %. perf record agrees: the reader's share of all samples went from 1.8 % to 4.4 %.
  • The card stops being the critical path. The busiest worker's wait for the card fell from 1015 to 392 us per pass at tp2 and from 351 to 264 us at tp4. The workers now finish their CPU work after the card more often, so the CPU-side growth reaches the step. At tp4 the card is fast even in base (351 us of wait per pass against 1015 at tp2), so almost all of the store and reader growth lands on the step: 566 + 374 = 940 us (est. sum) against the untraced tp4 loss of 800 to 1400 us per step. At tp2 with 8 users the card still hides most of it (untraced loss 148 us) unless the launch is forced late (810 us, block m2).
  • H2 (the staging gather) is real but hidden. On burst passes the GOF drain on the workers takes 7811 us (sum over workers) against 6757 us in base at tp2 (+1054 us, +3.7 us per populate; the benchmark predicted +2.0 us), and the main thread's coop drain grows from 55 to 91 us. Burst passes are not slower than quiet passes in the vnni arm (8.965 against 9.095 ms), so the drain overlaps the longer Save K window. H2 explains at most 0.05 ms per step on average (est.).
  • H4 (tp4 state) stays open. One trace per arm cannot separate the two tp4 modes; the m1b cells at tp4 (n = 2) are unresolved (sd 0.6 to 1.3 ms per step). The tp4 loss direction is clear in every run, its size is not.
  • The CI-like loads (block m1b, n = 2, untraced): tp2 with 2 users per engine, vnni minus headoff = +0.215 ms per step (-4.3 % TPS, t +43.8); tp2 with 4 users +0.131 ms (-2.2 %, t +17.8). headoff equals base at both loads. The 2-user cell reproduces the CI tp2 loss (-5.0 %) and the store prediction for 576 rows (0.20 ms).
  • Verdict. The root cause is the VNNI K layout's per-token access pattern in decode: 64 cache lines per row instead of 4, paid three times per token (the main-thread scatter store, the workers' tail read, the staging gather). Which of the three reaches the step depends on the launch phase and on the card's step length: with 2 users per engine (late launch) the store is fully exposed; at tp4 the workers' chain is the critical path and the store plus the tail read land on the step; at tp2 with 8 users the card hides most of it. The AMX kernel, the rest of the PR, and the card are not involved.
  • Implication for a fix (not measured): the three costs share one cause, the per-token column layout of an incomplete 16-token block. Storing the newest, incomplete block row-major (4 lines per row, dotter for the tail, contiguous rows for staging) and converting a block to the VNNI layout once its 16 tokens are complete (the prefill block store already exists) would remove all three decode costs while keeping the AMX operand layout for dense pages. The launch-phase switch (TRON_HWATTN_EARLY_LAUNCH_MIN_B=1) is a run-time mitigation for the 2-users-per-engine CI cell (+4.3 % TPS for the row-major binary itself, block m2) and should be confirmed in the CI layout (block m6).

  • The early-launch knob removes the CI tp2 loss and more. With TRON_HWATTN_EARLY_LAUNCH_MIN_B=1 the PR package gains 11.0 % TPS at 2 users (t -27, resolved) and then beats the nightly package as shipped by 6.2 %. The knob also speeds up the nightly package itself by 7.7 % (t -9.4): at 2 users per engine the default late launch leaves the card idle during Save K and Save V for every binary. When both packages launch early, the layout's remaining cost is +0.067 ms per step (-1.4 %), the same size as the runtron early-launch cells.
  • tp4 has a second, AMX-side component. With the kill switch the PR package recovers 6.8 % of its 13.4 % loss at 4 users (t -3.4, n = 2; the runtron m1 cells showed the same: vnnikill 8.1 % above vnni at tp4, t -3.1, and nothing at tp2). The AMX dense kernel itself barely runs in FPGA decode (0.04 % of samples), so the candidate is the per-call AMX preparation on the attention workers (Q-group packing and the tile-configuration bracket around every apply_page_range call, which the kill switch removes) landing on the critical path at tp4, where the workers' chain is the bottleneck. This component is not in the prediction matrix; it needs its own test: headoff with and without the kill switch at tp4 (both row-major), plus per-core frequency during the pending section (turbostat) to check for an AMX frequency-license effect. Label: hypothesis, unmeasured.
