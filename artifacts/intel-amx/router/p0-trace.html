Findings the cost model could not predict

  • The L3 floor is ~2.4× deeper than nominal. The rampup doc's napkin bound put the L3-served case at ≥10.9 µs/layer for n=128 (25 B/cycle per-core assumption); measured cyclic delivery is 26.0 µs. Sol round 1's refusal to accept the instruction-bound 6.9 µs number was right, and the correction goes further than the floor estimate.
  • Cache disruption adds nothing measurable (≤1% on every cell) — a 1 GiB streaming disruptor on a same-socket core does not move router-weight delivery. Capacity fit is reported separately from this observed behavior per the plan.
  • The license clock is not the deciding factor. Single-core effective clocks during 20 s steady runs: avx_fp32 3.89 GHz, avx_bf16 3.20 GHz, amx_bf16 3.59 GHz (package ~210 W throughout). The dense vdpbf16ps loop clocks LOWER than AMX and still ties it at M=1 n=32.
  • Multi-core split point: 4 workers over N at n=128 cyclic: amx 8.49 µs (M=8; 3.1× scaling), avx_bf16 24.36, avx_fp32 42.26. n=32: amx needs 16-column granularity per worker, so the split point is 2 workers: 5.39 µs (M=8). Barrier and orchestration overhead is inside these numbers.

For the FPGA team (option-e pricing)

The round trip a card-side fused router would remove: logits land on the host 21–22 µs (1u) / 34–36 µs (8u) after dispatch; the first expert hardware submission trails dispatch by 32–50 µs. If card-side fusion chains rmsnorm→router→top-k→expert dispatch without host involvement, the recoverable window is bounded by segment (c) minus the top-k/routing host work: roughly 25–45 µs per layer est — realizable only to the extent the FPGA idles in that window (not measurable from host-side spans; needs device occupancy). Traces: exec/results/router/*.perfetto-trace.

For Note [Expert matmuls] owners (segment (b) evidence)

After logits are ready, top-k + routing build + expert prepare cost only 6–10 µs per layer on the driving thread; the expert dispatch chain is not the bottleneck in these decode configs at tp2. The worker-0 competition risk the plan carried applies to a future CPU router placement (P1a's scheduling check), not to today's path.

5. What a CPU router would and would not buy est

The corrected-gate model, made explicit (all inputs measured, composition is a model):

  • Today per layer (1u, 20b): the driving thread launches (+1.4 µs), then blocks 19.3 µs for the top-k output, whose critical input is the router round trip (logits at +21.4).
  • CPU-world per layer: compute logits in-place (best arm 6.4 µs), helpers still enter at ≈E = 10.6, top-k done ≈11; residual block ≈ max(0, E+SP − (tcpu+2)) = 2.6 µs. Per-layer serial cost drops ≈11.7 µs → 280 µs/step = +5.4% (matches the bootstrap mean).
  • At 8u the block is larger (32.4) and AMX stays flat (6.57), so the saving grows in absolute terms but T_step doubles: +2.6–3.6%.
  • 120b single-core: tcpu=26 µs eats the entire 19–33 µs block at 1u (net negative). The measured 4-worker AMX kernel (8.49 µs) would flip 120b positive (≈+3–5% est) at the price of cross-core orchestration — exactly the Note [Expert matmuls] worker-contention risk; P1a's scheduling/FPGA-feed check decides.
  • Not counted for the gate (conservative): removing launch-side work (≈1.7 µs/layer), and any FPGA-utilization gain from freeing the router matmul slot.

Deviations and gaps (Rule 12: surfaced, not hidden)

  • Gate-variable mis-specification (the deviation): the pre-registration named the logits-read slice; the block actually lands at the top-k output read one statement later. Both computations are reported everywhere; the pre-registered result is STOP, the corrected result is the PROCEED-eligible row. Endorsed by jhan 2026-08-24 after a Sol consensus round (exec/results/router/sol-deviation-review.md; decision package router/G0-decision-package.md).
  • Independent recomputation (exec/r0-g0-recompute.py, session 7ff0b556): reproduced (A_corr/T exactly; g pre-reg within 0.02pp); a stricter per-layer residual clamp changes no verdict (worst move: 120b 1u/2K −1.42% → −1.62%, still negative). The published g0-decision*.json stay as decided; per-layer clamping applies prospectively in the P1a pre-registration. Closes Sol finding 8.
  • Missing-records note: 7/5880 (20b 1u/2K) and 8/216 (120b 1u/2K) layer records lack helper_entry_off (first-wait slice absent in the window); they contribute to A but not to E. Immaterial here; the P1a pre-registration treats this case explicitly.
  • (a-roundtrip) via perfetto flow join: not extracted. Flow edges connect consecutive hops, not launch→rx; a correct extraction needs a chain walk. The consumer-side arrival is covered by the helper first-wait metric instead.
  • 120b traces are truncated (in-process tracer limits): usable steps 6 / 46 / 166 / 41 per config. The 1u/2K CI is correspondingly wide; conclusions for 120b lean on the better-covered cells.
  • Prefill capture needed the new TRON_TRACE_GEN_FROM_PARSE knob; the stock whole-run --trace cannot capture prefill (its 5 s cap expires during model load) and produced a 773-byte file on the first attempt.
  • amx n=32 with 4 workers is not measurable (8 columns/worker < 16-column tile granularity); the bench refuses it, and the 2-worker point stands in.
  • Insufficient data: FPGA idle time inside the dispatch→expert-submission window (decides how much of the option-e bound is real). Resolution: device-side occupancy measurement or the FPGA team's own counters against these traces.

7. Glossary

decode step / T_step
One token-generation iteration of the work queue for a q_batch; measured launch-to-launch of layer 0's router matmul.
M
Decode tokens in the batch per step (1u→1, 8u→8; prefill chunks up to 128). Router weight is shared across tokens, so the kernel runs once per layer at width M.
driving thread
Worker 0; runs the per-layer dispatch sequence (fill/prepare/launch, routing, expert prepare). Its blocked time is serial cost for the whole step.
stall / block
Time a thread waits inside a channel start_reading/start_writing (latch wait). Dashed in the lanes diagram.
cyclic regime
Bench walks all layers' weights round-robin (model-accurate working set), vs l2 (one hot layer) and disrupted (cyclic + a same-socket streaming thread).
license clock
Core frequency reduction under wide-vector/AMX instruction load; measured with turbostat on the pinned core.
tp2
Tensor-parallel over the machine's 2 FPGAs.
G0
The pre-registered stop/go gate: proceed only if predicted net decode gain clears +1% (upper 95% CI) in some target config.
