Inside the exactly-cut fence windows: both lottery shapes sit in the logits-stream leg

What was asked and what was found

The fence campaign found two launch-lottery shapes in one campaign: a stall-mode draw (draw_04: baseline median window, 5.7% of windows >1.5 ms) and level-shifted draws (draw_10/06/15: median window up ~80–150µs, almost no stalls). This follow-up cuts each window exactly at the fence instants and asks: which named spans carry each shape?

Answer: the same leg carries both. The [F1→F3] window is almost entirely waiting on the streamed logits (named host work inside it is ~7.5µs flat, all-idle ~10–13µs flat). Every window belongs to one of three populations: baseline (~283µs median), a recurring ~800µs mode whose extra time is 82% fill_logits_buffer (logits stream wait+consume: +416 of +508µs), and rare severe stalls (>1.5 ms, fill-dominated, +1.3 ms). The launch lottery sets the occupancy of the elevated populations: level-shifted draws double the ~800µs-mode share (fast ~21% → 38–44% of the 350–800µs band), the stall-mode draw adds 5.7% severe stalls instead. The dispatch edge stays flat in every shape (F1→listener start ~11µs, first wcls data-ready +12–17µs).

The “level shift” is not a uniform per-window slowdown: p25 moves little (+53µs) while p75 jumps (403→784µs) — the elevated draws move windows INTO the ~800µs mode rather than stretching every window. draw_15 shows the mixture most clearly: its median fill is even lower than the fast draws’ (215.7 vs ~237µs) while 44% of its windows sit in the 350–800µs band — the median leg values stay near baseline, the occupancy does the damage. Leg medians (window start → listener job start; listener end → F3) are flat in every draw, confirming the fence campaign’s flat data-ready finding from the span side.

How this composes with the earlier results

The gated-window campaign (2026-08-12-07 page) measured the whole inter-fwd-pass window and found bimodal windows whose mixture the launch sets, with everything co-stretching inside stalled windows. The fence cut isolates the core leg and sharpens this: within [F1→F3] the host-work co-stretch contribution is negligible (~7.5µs of work either way) — the population differences are almost purely fill_logits_buffer, i.e. waiting for and consuming the streamed logits. The two shapes previously seen on the two hosts (3c51 drift / born-elevated; 3bda stall-mixture) are one mechanism observed at two severities: mild (~800µs, +416µs of stream time) and severe (>1.5 ms stalls). Note the co-stretch of next-step prep measured on 2026-08-12 sits BETWEEN F3 and the next F1, outside these windows — it remains real but is a second-order effect (the F1→F3 leg alone gives per-draw mean corr −0.85 vs TPS per the fence campaign).

Next collection target suggested by this cut

Per-block stream-arrival stamps, scoped to ~800µs-mode windows. The ~800µs mode is 82% fill (stream wait+consume) with the dispatch edge flat, so the open question is now precise: within an ~800µs-mode window, do the logits blocks arrive late from the card/DMA (arrival gaps), or arrive on time and get consumed slowly? The existing per-block stamp plan (step-4 yellow box) answers it directly, and the fence instants make the target windows trivially identifiable online. A cheap pre-check on existing data: kvprobe4’s B1/B2 split showed the tail-after-last-completion carries stall losses; whether that also holds for the mild mode is testable from these traces by comparing the last wcls “data ready” offset (flat, ~12–17µs) against fill END offsets — already extracted per window.
