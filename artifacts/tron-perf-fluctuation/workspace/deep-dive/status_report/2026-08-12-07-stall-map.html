Stall map: the inter-fwd-pass window on every thread, and what changes when it stalls

Same visual language as the concurrency map: one lane per concurrent thread, numbered circles ①–⑧ for the causal chain, every term mapped to source (trunk 12804a812; line numbers grep-verified, see the step-5 appendix). Thread names are the literal perfetto/perf names. Both panels are drawn at the SAME time scale (0.5 px per µs) from two real measured windows, so the stall’s size is visually honest.

What this page illustrates: once per token, after layer 35, all threads converge on one dense ~590µs window (lm-head → streamed logits → token pick → next dispatch). In a stalled window (>1500µs) nothing new happens — the SAME sequence runs, but every station stretches together: the card’s stream chunks pause ~200µs, the TX waits grow, and pure host compute runs ×3–5 slower at constant clock (2.70 GHz, verified at 150µs resolution). A launch draws only how OFTEN this happens: 5.7% of windows in the fastest draw, 34.6% in the slowest — that rate IS the lottery.

  3. TX threads dispatch. Each card’s TX thread runs tx_launch: waits for activations (act_ready.wait), marks ▲ data ready, submits the activation DMA and rings the doorbell (worker.cpp:2127; data-ready marker added right after act_ready.wait).
  4. Listener worker enters fill. 3.5 · Is the endpoint a sync point? Yes — via a fence chain, not at run_attention_job itself

By the time any worker enters run_attention_job() for the next pass, every thread’s previous-pass work is provably complete — but the rendezvous happens earlier, through three fences in series. Two of them are definitive sync points (FENCE 1 and FENCE 3); FENCE 2 is not (the listener’s fill/callback is concurrent with the final RX completion). Source-verified 2026-08-13: launch_wcls runs strictly AFTER plugin_state.run() returns, so at FENCE 1 the hardware pipeline is EMPTY — the no-definitive-point gap is exactly [F1→F3], the streaming region, concurrent by design. Because there is exactly ONE work-queue thread, the whole machine funnels through it between the fences and the endpoint:

Guarantees at the endpoint: all 70 pool workers retired the previous pass (FENCE 1, latch counted to full pool width); the previous pass’s last hardware job (wcls) completed through the card AND its RX completion callback (FENCE 2) — so no previous-pass TX/RX work can exist; the token callback returned and every listener task acknowledged (FENCE 3). Not quiescent, by design: TX/RX are already busy with NEXT-pass dispatches (the layer-0 WQ matmul must finish before the endpoint’s Q-wait can return), and µs-scale RX descriptor cleanup may trail FENCE 2. The window-OPEN edge (layer-35 barrier) is NOT a global sync — only attention workers rendezvous there; W_O/MLP/wcls overlap it.

Why this matters for the lottery: the span is bounded by a worker rendezvous at open and this fence chain at close, so ANY thread’s lateness anywhere in the step is absorbed into this one span — the structural reason the window carries 96.7% of the launch difference, and why every co-stretch symptom lands here regardless of cause.

Why FENCE 3 is not the span END (but is the right internal cut): fence 3 is a definitive sync point (project term, adopted 2026-08-13: an instant every operation on every thread is definitively before or after — nothing concurrent with it; CS equivalents: quiescent point, inevitable global state, BSP barrier). The span endpoint is NOT one — TX’s next-pass dispatches are concurrent with it. Fence 3 is the definitive cross-thread rendezvous, yet real once-per-token work follows it — the scheduler’s serial segment (extend_request, compute_forward_args ×3–5 in stalls, construct_minibatches ×12, plan, enqueue) — and that segment stretches with the lottery (TSC segment C.rest: 421k→526k cycles fast4→slow4, corr −0.69). Ending the span at fence 3 would push that loss outside the measured window and break the step reconciliation. Instead, fence 3 splits the span into two phases with a deterministic boundary: phase 1, open→fence 3 = finish the previous step (collective, ≈A1′+B′+callback); phase 2, fence 3→endpoint = construct the next step (single-threaded, ≈C.rest). The cut is now MEASURED (tag 7600, campaign 3c51-fence3-tp4-20260813T050910Z, 16/16, token hash unchanged, zero freeze holes): cb→F3 is FLAT (23.0k→21.6k cycles fast4→slow4, corr +0.11, median ~0.8k) — the scheduler’s wake at the definitive sync point is excluded as a suspect; F3→endpoint carries the entire stretch (410k→521k cycles, corr −0.79; replicates kvprobe7’s C.rest). The FENCE-3-to-FENCE-3 per-token period reconciles with TPS-derived step time to 0.01%.

Fence-based attribution with ZERO residual (tag 7601+7600, campaign 3c51-fence13-tp4-20260813T060034Z, 16/16, hash unchanged ×17): the span growth (+294k cycles fast4→slow4) tiles exactly into open→F1 +176k (corr −0.65) · F1→wcls-dispatch +23k (corr −0.81, small but the most tightly correlated child) · wcls-dispatch→F3 +71k (corr −0.52) · F3→endpoint +24k — residual 0. Two standing findings: (1) the 70-worker join convoy (open→F1) moves with the lottery — codex finding F1’s convoy is a first-class carrier, not just fill texture; (2) the F3-wake exclusion replicated (cb→F3 flat, corr −0.10). Composition caveat: two campaigns 50 minutes apart expressed the SAME lottery through DIFFERENT legs — fill-dominated at 05:09 (B +275k of +516k) vs join-convoy-dominated at 06:00 (open→F1 +176k of +294k, B only +60k) — the familiar co-stretch-with-varying-shares texture: the manifestation moves between waiting legs; the shared cause sits upstream of all of them.

3.6 · Fence 1 and Fence 3 in source — and why they are the definitive sync points

Both snippets are verbatim from the current probe worktree (trunk 12804a812 + the instrumentation series; the tag-7601/7600 stamps were added by the trace-sync-points session and are the measured fence instants: each record’s t0+dur IS the fence timestamp).

FENCE 1 — workers_done.wait() returning inside plugin_state.run()

gen/src/tron/h/tron/plugins/qwen_3_4b_instruct_2507.hpp (generated plugin), grep-pinned; caller sequence at model.hpp:1784–1795

q_batch->linear_channel_5.start_reading(0);
kernel_add_rmsnorm_71(q_batch, 0, n_workers);      // final norm — the pass’s LAST host kernel
q_batch->linear_channel_5.finish_reading(0);
// All the workers we spawned must be done before run() returns.
// See Note [Plugin-owned worker launch] in model.hpp.
if (kvwait_probe::state().enabled) {
  const uint64_t f1_t0 = kvwait_probe::state_t::now();
  workers_done.wait();                             // <- FENCE 1: return = definitive sync point
  kvwait_probe::state().add(f1_t0,
      kvwait_probe::state_t::now() - f1_t0, 7601);  // site 7601: Fence 1
}

Why its return instant is a definitive sync point — every operation class lands provably on one side:

  • All 70 pool workers: before. workers_done is a latch counted to the FULL pool width (codex S4); every attention and helper task of the pass must count down before the wait can return. The latch uses acquire/release atomics (threading.hpp:124), so these are true happens-before edges.
  • All hardware: before. The pass’s last matmul results (layer-35 W_O/MLP) were consumed by the helper kernels that precede the count-downs — the final norm sits directly above the wait in the snippet — and the next hardware job (wcls) is launched only AFTER run() returns (run_forward: run() → enqueue listeners → logits_prepared.wait() → launch_wcls). The pipeline is empty at the fence instant.
  • All listener work: after. Listener tasks are enqueued only after run() returns — they do not exist yet.
  • Single observer. The wait returns on the one work-queue thread, which is also the only thread that creates any subsequent work — nothing can start “next” work concurrently with the fence.

FENCE 3 — listeners_notified.wait() returning in run_forward

h/tron/models/model.hpp:3082–3090; the matching count-down side is the listener’s listeners_notified.count_down() at :3181, AFTER the token callback (tag 7400, :3177–3181) and tree cleanup

// This is the main thread waiting for all of the callbacks to be done.
if (kvwait_probe::state().enabled) {
  const uint64_t f3_t0 = kvwait_probe::state_t::now();
  listeners_notified.wait();                       // <- FENCE 3: return = definitive sync point
  kvwait_probe::state().add(f3_t0,
      kvwait_probe::state_t::now() - f3_t0, 7600);  // site 7600: Fence 3
}
  • All listener work: before. The latch is counted to the full listener-task width; each task counts down only after its token callback returned and its tree-lock cleanup finished (codex S12–S14).
  • All hardware: before. The scheduler reached this wait only after logits_done (FENCE 2 — the wcls job’s final RX completion callback), and nothing new is dispatched until the scheduler proceeds past this line. The pipeline is again empty at the fence instant.
  • All next-step work: after. Pool workers are parked; extend_request, compute_forward_args and the next dispatches all execute on this same thread after the return.
  • Single observer, as at FENCE 1.

The convention, and the contrast with FENCE 2. “Definitive” is claimed under two stated exemptions: idle/poll loops (TX/RX dequeue spins, parked workers — they span every instant by construction and touch no semantic state) and the µs-scale RX descriptor cleanup that can trail a completion callback. Two caveats stay on record: codex F4/F9 show some intended edges are volatile rather than atomic (operationally ordered on this x86 binary, formally data races), and FENCE 2 fails the definition outright — at the instant the final RX callback fires, the listener’s fill/consume/callback work is typically still running, so operations exist on both sides of it concurrently. That is why the fence-based attribution cuts at F1 and F3, never at F2.

Glossary

  • window / inter-fwd-pass window — the once-per-token dense span between forward passes (① to ⑧ above).
  • stalled window — a window whose span exceeds 1500µs (the valley between the two duration populations).
  • A1′ / B′ / C′ — window legs: open→fill-start, fill, fill-end→close (primed to distinguish them from the older TSC-probe segments A1/B/C which have slightly different edges).
  • corr — Pearson correlation coefficient between a per-draw quantity and that draw’s decode TPS across all draws; −1 = grows exactly when the launch is slower, 0 = unrelated.
  • NaN latch — the 0xffff sentinel the host writes at each result block’s tail; the card’s DMA overwriting it is the block’s arrival signal.
  • data ready (▲) — instant marker on the TX thread when a job’s activations become ready, just before its DMA submit.
  • definitive sync point — an instant that every operation on every thread is definitively before or after (nothing concurrent with it; convention: idle/poll loops and µs-scale trailing rx cleanup exempted). The token cycle has TWO: FENCE 1 (workers_done → 0: hardware empty, wcls not yet launched — source-verified 2026-08-13, correcting an earlier “scoped-only” assessment) and FENCE 3 (listeners_notified return). Neither span edge is one. The irreducible gap without one is [F1→F3] — the streaming region, concurrent by design. CS names: quiescent point, inevitable global state, BSP superstep barrier.
  • draw — one full launch of runtron; the lottery is drawn per launch.
